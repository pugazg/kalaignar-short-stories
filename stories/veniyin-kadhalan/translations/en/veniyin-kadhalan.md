# Veniyin Kadhalan

It was eight at night. In the women’s ward of the military hospital, Nurse Surya was attending busily to her duties. Yet it was easy to see that her thoughts were absorbed in something else, swaying around it.

Lines of worry lay plainly across the youthful beauty of her face. As she treated and cared for the sick women, her lovely red lips kept murmuring: “O Lord! Somehow save him! I live only for him. Even if he refuses my living love today, one day he is bound to accept it. Until then I shall wait. Until then, let him live! And after that, let us both live!”

That was the poor woman’s prayer. In the middle of it, military Sergeant Gundappan entered. Nurse Surya respectfully welcomed him.

“How is Veni?” Gundappan asked.

“Completely well—she only needs good rest. That is all!” Surya replied.

Still talking, Gundappan went and stood by Veni’s bed. “Veni!” he called. Veni, who had been groaning in illness, shut her eyes and turned to the other side as soon as she saw him. She had lain unconscious for two or three days and had opened her eyes only that day. The way she turned away on seeing Gundappan surprised Surya, though she did not show it. Gundappan too managed the situation. Saying to Surya, “All right, I’ll be going,” he signalled that he had some secret to tell her and called her aside. Surya waited eagerly to hear it.

“Surya! Be careful with Veni! She may even run away!” he said, and hurried away. Surya understood nothing.

Run away? Why? What for had the sergeant said that? Then who was this woman? Surya became a slave to questions like these. Confused and unable to work, she went and sat by her desk. Her thoughts multiplied. She turned toward Veni’s cot and started. Veni was not there. She was just then slipping away from the ward. Surya sprang after her, caught her, and demanded, “Where are you running?”

“Amma—please let me go. You will earn a crore of blessings.” The young woman wept.

“Let you go without the sergeant’s permission? I can’t, child, I can’t!”

“The sergeant—who is he to give me orders, Amma? The noble man who has the right to command me lies there struck mute, Mother!

“If you wish, give me a little poison—but do not hand me over to that viper.”

“What fine justice! Is this how you deceive someone who trusts you? How much trouble Gundappan takes so that your body may recover!”

“Yes, my body is what he wants. That is why he takes trouble. Why do people protect a fruit from rotting? Out of compassion for it? No—because they want to taste and eat it! This deceiver belongs to that kind, Amma!”

“Why do you flare up at him? Aren’t you his kept woman?” Surya asked.

“Ayyo! Don’t say that, Amma! Do not say it even once more! Listen, Mother, to my wretched tale. Holy woman who watches over bodies ruined by wounds, listen to the history of my life, torn to rags by the savage games of a madman. There is a soldier in the army.......he is my beloved husband. The sinner Gundappan torments him beyond endurance and tries to drag me to his bed. He is only a soldier, Mother, but he is a man of compassion! He is the life mingled with my breath. Before my own eyes that brute attacked him. My head spun. Crying ‘Kanda! Kanda!’ I fell to the ground. That is all I know. When I woke, I was in the hospital.”

“Kandan!”

“Yes, Amma! Why—do you know him?”

“The man who recently received a flogging punishment in the army—is that him?”

“Yes, Mother. That moon is the song of my life’s ideal. That wicked man beat him until he sagged—beat him until he sagged. The shoulders on which I used to cling and play—

—the hands that used to cover my eyes in play were themselves toyed with and torn by the tip of that outcaste’s whip. Blood ran from his eyes. That cruel man did not stop, Amma—right in front of him he fondled me. He spoke obscene words. He said he would make me a caged bird. That is why he is in such a hurry, Mother. That is why he is so concerned about my health. Amma, let me go! If I were your own sister, would you not tremble at the sight of a single tear? Amma...save me. Otherwise I will not leave you!” Veni pleaded. Surya’s mind reeled; her eyes drooped.

Kandan—now Veni’s husband—had once captivated Surya’s heart. In those days Surya’s family floated in wealth. Prosperous and distinguished bridegrooms waited at the doorway of her house. How could the daughter of that wealthy household garland poverty-stricken Kandan? On an auspicious day fixed by the priest, Surya became the wife of the son of Kuttippatti Mudaliar.

Surya had married before the sacred fire. At the wedding pavilion she stepped upon the grinding stone and beheld Arundhati. What of it? Within ten months Surya had become a widow. Poor Surya! Did the storm around her stop there? Her father spent all their property and comforts on the steps of the High Court. Was that all? The wealth that had come by a crooked path went away by a crooked path without telling anyone, and in grief over that he too died.

After burying her father, Surya joined nursing school the very next day. In those days she had not even dreamed that she might meet Kandan, that rare medicine of her heart, or wander in a Gandharva world. Do things ever happen as a person imagines?

Kandan came to the hospital unconscious. He entered Surya’s memory again.

And at just this time Veni had to spring up as a rival for her love! Who was this Veni? How had she become Kandan’s wife? Surya did want to ask and learn all this. But she did not wish to increase her own pain by questioning her.

Kandan, who had slept upon her soft shoulders—was another woman, Veni, to claim rights over him? Surya could not bear even to think of Veni as a rival in love.

There was only one way for Surya to renew her dreams of desire. Yes...she could give Veni poison as though it were medicine and kill her.... Then Kandan would forget Veni.... Afterwards Surya could occupy that place....

A wonderful idea indeed! But Surya’s gentle heart shrank before the dreadful thought. Was the nursing profession, raised high by the great service of the Florence Nightingales, to be stained merely for a love affair?

Surya’s heart became a battlefield. Without her knowing it, her lips murmured:

“Let her live.... Let Veni’s life receive new life!” Thus spoke that emblem of sacrifice.

Her blooming red-lotus face tightened like a closed lily bud. Under the lightning assault of sorrow, her lips darkened and cracked.

“Get up! Do not be troubled. I am the one treating him,” Surya consoled her in a voice soaked with grief.

At these words from Surya, a shade of happiness spread across Veni’s face. “How is he now, Amma? Does he speak? Did he say my name—Veni?”

“Yes. One day he moaned your name.”

“He moaned! My king moaned! Where is he now, Amma?”

“He is in the military hospital itself. I too must go there shortly to treat him!”

“Amma, take good care of him. Give me those hands that treat the man who has become a picture in my heart—give them to me, Amma, give them!” Veni took Nurse Surya’s flower-like hands and pressed them to her eyes.

“Veni!” Surya breathed the name from the depths of her heart with the help of a great sigh. A tear budding at the corner of her sidelong eye fell upon her breast.

“Come, Veni! Come, I will show you that king who fills your eyes!” Surya cried, seizing Veni’s hand and pulling her along. At that moment a trolley was coming from the men’s ward.

“My, Mr Damodar, which ‘bed’ patient is this? What happened?” Surya asked the man pushing the trolley.

“This is the very fellow you took pity on and guarded night and day, Surya! The man is ‘finished.’”

“Ah! Kandan? Veni! The man who entered our hearts is dead, Veni!”

Surya collapsed to the floor.

“Ayyo, Gundappa, outcaste who cut life itself apart! Kanda! Kanda! Kanda!” Veni fell upon the trolley—her head broke open, blood poured out and reddened the floor. In that crimson blood her black hair lay soaked.
