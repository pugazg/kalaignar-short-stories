# Why Did the Warrior Bow His Head?

<!-- source scan 36; printed page 35 -->

Those gathered in that hall that day belonged to a particular group.

They were the sort who wasted time conducting an inquiry into everyone in the town. That day too, competing with one another, they sat talking about the town. Speaking contemptuously of someone was their favourite pastime. If enmity entered the town and its army assembled, this group would run away and hide. When the enemy was defeated and the town’s soldiers celebrated victory, those who had hidden would return and hold idle discussions about how the victory had been won. They had no enthusiasm for the work of the town, nor any interest in acts of courage that defended it. Their only passion was to spend their time in empty chatter.

People who passed that hall on their own business paid them no attention. Merchants went that way. Warriors walked that way. Beautiful women passed that way. None of them looked at the group or showed them respect. Each simply went on with his or her own work.

Yet, for some reason, on that day a warrior bowed his head as he crossed the hall.

His legs faltered. Old age had not afflicted him; it had not even begun to rule over him. Illness had not approached him. He had mountain-like shoulders. His eyes shone like rays of light. He was an iron statue

walking. On the battlefield he was like a tiger when breaking the waist of the enemy clan.

Why, then, did I writhe like a worm when that mighty warrior crossed the hall? Was it because his heart, like a wisp in a storm wind, made him creep away like a cat?

The mere look from his wide eyes could make that group, whose profession was to speak contemptuously of everyone, scream and scatter.

But where was that proud gaze?

Where was that majestic stride?.... No one answered. Even the practitioners of ridicule gathered in the hall, not knowing the reason for the warrior’s weakened gait, invented many stories.

His own conscience tells the true reason.

Though he possessed every kind of fame, he had not been blessed with a wife who wished to protect that fame. That is why, when he passes before people whose occupation is to speak degradingly of others, his heart pierces him. His stride weakens. His head bows.
