# What Unites the Two Is the Aathi Garland

<!-- source scan 15; printed page 14 -->

Kovur Kizhar, a Sangam-age poet, was a friend of the two Cholas Nalangilli and Nedungilli. One day Ilanthathan, Nalangilli's court poet, comes to Nedungilli.

Nedungilli suspects, ‘He is not a poet. He is a spy sent by Nalangilli.’

Kovur Kizhar immediately intervenes. “No, no, Nedungilli! Do not be hasty. He is indeed a poet! This Ilanthathan is a poet who works in Nalangilli's *kozhu mandapam*,” he says, and saves him.

The next stage — Chola Nalangilli wages war on Chola Nedungilli, who was in Uraiyur. Do you know what part Kovur Kizhar plays in that war?

Like Krishna — like Krishna who went as an envoy for the Pandavas — he does not ask, give this, give that.

<!-- source scan 16; printed page 15 -->

He stands between the two kings and sings. What does he sing?

“Your garland upon your breast is the same;
The garland upon the breast of the one who fights you is the same.”

That is: “O Chola, Nedungilli, what you wear is Aathi; Nalangilli too wears Aathi; both of you belong to the Chola lineage.”

“If either one of you loses, it is your clan that loses.”

History says that whichever of the two loses, it is the Chola clan that loses.

“For both of you to win is not in the nature of things.”

It is not natural for both of them to emerge victorious. Therefore,

“What you are doing does not befit the honour of your clan.”

Thus he sings that their action is not worthy of the dignity of their clan.
