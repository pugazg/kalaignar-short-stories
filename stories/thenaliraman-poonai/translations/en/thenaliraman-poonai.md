# Tenali Raman's Cat

> **English translation of the verified Tamil archival reading.** Controlling source: 1987 collection `கலைஞர் சொன்ன குட்டிக் கதைகள்`, lower scan **46 / printed page 45 → scan 47 / printed page 46 → upper scan 48 / printed page 47**.

Voting rights for those who have completed 18 years! Reserved places for mothers! Reserved places will be allotted in local councils for the oppressed people!

Such sweet announcements! M.G.R. made all three of these announcements! But did he even do that properly? Did he do it in an orderly way? No.

Was it because he did not know? I do not believe so. He knows! It was deliberately done in an irregular manner. If it was done that way, someone would go to court! Through the court, a stay on the election would come. Then, with a sigh, the election could simply be left unheld. It was an act deliberately carried out for that purpose.

<!-- anthology scan: 47; printed page: 46 -->

We have heard a story that Tenali Raman raised a cat. The king gave Tenali Raman a beautiful kitten and told him to raise it until it was big and return it to him after one year. The king also handed over gold and coins for raising the kitten and waited.

Do you know what Tenali Raman did on the first day? He brought the kitten home and set out milk for it. What kind of milk, do you know? Good, hot milk—boiling milk so hot that if the tongue touched it, the tongue would burn and curl up—placed in a bowl before the kitten. At the sight of the milk, the kitten was overjoyed. Just as the oppressed rejoiced when told there would be reserved places for the oppressed! Just as mothers rejoiced when told there would be reserved places for mothers! Just as eighteen-year-old youths rejoiced when told they would all have voting rights! In the same way, the kitten rejoiced.

“Ah, milk!” It put its mouth to it. Its mouth burned, its tongue burned, and the cat ran away. The next day he brought the same kitten back and set out milk that had cooled well.

Would it come near? It was a cat that had been scalded, so it would not. It ran away as if saying, “I won't even turn back to look at milk.” Later, even though the kitten grew big, it remained thin, all bone and skin.

The king summoned Tenali Raman. “Where is the kitten I told you to raise? Bring it,” he said. Tenali Raman brought it. It was all bone and skin. “What is this? I gave you thousands of gold coins and told you to raise it! Why is the cat all bone and skin like this?” the king asked. Tenali Raman replied, “I put out milk for this cat every single day. But it simply does not drink it.”

<!-- anthology scan: 48; printed page: 47; source span: upper -->

“A cat that does not drink milk? What a wonder this is in the whole world!” exclaimed the astonished king. He ordered milk from his own palace to be brought and placed directly before the cat.

The moment it saw the milk, the cat bolted away on all fours. “All right, what Tenali Raman says is true,” the king believed.

It is just the same here. Just as the cat that had been scalded—the cat raised by Tenali Raman—ran away even when it later saw cooled milk, the people of this country were shown the milk called “reserved places for the oppressed,” “reserved places for mothers,” and “voting rights for all eighteen-year-old youths”—but were shown it as scalding milk—until they were made to say, “Oh! Father! We don't want milk at all.” The distinction of bringing this very election to such a conclusion can belong to no one other than my dear friend, the Honourable Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu, M.G. Ramachandran.
