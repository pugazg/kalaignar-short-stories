## Difficult / source-sensitive choices

The persistent `POSSIBLE_ERRORS_FOR_REVIEW.md` queue was read before translation. The English follows the verified Tamil rather than silently replacing unusual source forms.

- `களித்துப்போய்` was rendered by immediate emotional sense as the narrator becoming **spent**, without rewriting the Tamil source form.
- the unusual source pronoun `அவள் உள்ளத்தில்` was deliberately preserved in English as **“in her heart”**, rather than silently changing it to “his”.
- `ஐதர் காலத்துத் தையல் மிஷின்தான்` was rendered conservatively as **Hyder-era sewing machine**.
- the tailoring/daughter wordplay around `அவன் பெற்ற தையல்` was translated without altering the Tamil source; the English keeps the turn from literal sewing to Maragatham as the “one he produced”.
- `முழுங்கால்` was rendered contextually as **knee-deep**, while the Tamil itself remains untouched.
- `சன சுரத்தை` was translated by its immediate rhetorical function as the **soft note** competing with the louder inner voice; no Tamil normalization was made.
- `மூனையளவு` was represented conservatively as **the little measure** of youth she has.
- `மண்ணுக்கி` was rendered as **bury ... in the dust**, preserving the sentence’s force without rewriting the source.
- `மழலை பருகிடவேண்டிய தாய்ப்பால் மருந்து பருகியது` was kept source-close as **the mother’s milk meant for the infant was swallowed by medicine**, rather than replacing the image with a medical explanation not stated in the text.
- `‘பெட்காபி’` was kept as **‘bed coffee’**.
- the difficult insult `எமை விட்டு எச்சில் இலையே!` was translated minimally as **“Not even spit is left for us!”**; no speculative emendation was introduced.
- `டி.பி.—எலும்புருக்கி` was represented as **T.B.—the bone-wasting disease**, preserving both the abbreviation and the source gloss.

## Structural and rhetorical review

- the narrator’s opening reverence for his elderly mother frames the question of what truly constitutes motherhood;
- the mother’s story deliberately contrasts Maragatham, who cares for and nurses both children, with Soundari, who gave birth but rejects nursing in order to preserve youth and beauty;
- the animal-mother observations and the two competing inner voices remain the rhetorical centre of Soundari’s decision;
- Sekar’s earlier fear that a poor wife would become a servant in his household is fulfilled after marriage and retained before his final revolt;
- the brothers’ gun confrontation is interrupted not by reconciliation but by Soundari’s collapse and diagnosis, preserving the source’s ironic ending;
- no outside medical, social or moral explanation was added.
