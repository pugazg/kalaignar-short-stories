# The Ascetic and the Disciples

> **English translation of the verified Tamil archival reading.** Controlling source: 1987 collection `கலைஞர் சொன்ன குட்டிக் கதைகள்`, lower scan **10 → 11 → upper 12**. The source's unusual wording is translated conservatively rather than silently repaired.

<!-- anthology scan: 10; printed page: 9; lower shared span -->

An ascetic ran a monastery with many students. He used to teach the students of the monastery good advice. One day the students held a discussion among themselves: “This ascetic—with whom?—we too have been staying here and studying for a long time. We like everything in this monastery. But the cook here does not prepare good food. In any case, if a woman were to come here as the cook, we would get good food.” Having decided this, all the students together met the head of the monastery.

They told him what they had in mind. The ascetic immediately said, “If a woman cook comes into this monastery and any one of you develops a wrong thought—desire—towards her, what will become of the purity of this monastery? Therefore I cannot agree to employ a woman cook.”

Some of the students immediately said, “Never mind. Employ an old woman as the cook. If you appoint one, the situation you speak of will not arise.” He replied, “All right, I will think about it and tell you tomorrow. Everyone go for the night—”

<!-- anthology scan: 11; printed page: 10 -->

“—meal,” he said.

The students finished their duties and came ready for dinner. Before that, the ascetic called the cook and ordered him, “Tonight put far too much salt in the food served to the students. And when the food is served, do not place water for anyone.” As the ascetic had instructed, food with excessive salt was served to the students and no water was set out for anyone.

The ascetic sat down and ate with them. No salt had been mixed into the food served to him. The students did not know this. Seeing the ascetic eat quickly, they were afraid: “The ascetic himself is eating like this! How can we say there is too much salt in our food?” So every one of them finished eating and then went to the lodging where they stayed and lay down. The guru went to his room. Before that he called the cook and said, “In the courtyard near the verandah where the students sleep, leave a vessel with cow dung mixed into water.” The cook did exactly that.

Because the students had eaten heavily salted food, they were tormented by thirst. They did not know what to do. The water pots that were normally kept there were absent. After searching and searching, the students finally saw the vessel in which dung had been mixed with water.

One student said, “After a little while the dung water will settle. After that, we shall take that water and—”

<!-- anthology scan: 12; printed page: 11; upper shared span -->

“—drink it,” and waited. As the dung slowly settled, the student took a cup, scooped up the water and drank it. When he scooped with the cup, the dung water became cloudy again.

But the students could not bear their thirst. With no other way, they took that very dung water, drank it, and quenched their thirst.

In the morning the ascetic came and asked, “What happened to the dung water that was here?” The students told him what had happened. He then said, “In your thirst, without even considering whether it was dung water or good water, you drank it. In the same way, even if an old woman were employed here, in your desire you might commit a wrong without even looking to see whether she was an old woman or a young maiden.”

This story is enough to explain how wicked desire can be.
