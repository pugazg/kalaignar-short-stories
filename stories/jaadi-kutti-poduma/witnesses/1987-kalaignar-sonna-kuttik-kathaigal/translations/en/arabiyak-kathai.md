# An Arabian Tale

> **English translation of the verified 1987 witness reading.** This translation belongs only to the 1987 `கலைஞர் சொன்ன குட்டிக் கதைகள்` witness for canonical `ஜாடி குட்டி போடுமா?`. The controlling 2008 Tamil and English remain unchanged. Physical source-page boundaries are retained for traceability.

<!-- anthology scan: 6; printed page: 5; lower shared span -->

Sometimes some people are honest in small matters, but they are not honest in large matters. If they borrow five rupees, the very next day they will return six rupees, with interest.

But if they borrow five thousand rupees, they will leave town and run away. Therefore, the honesty shown in a small matter must also be followed in a large matter.

There is an Arabian tale that people tell. A man borrowed a jar from his neighbour. It was a small jar. A week later he took it back and returned it. When he returned it, he gave a little jar along with that jar. The man who had lent the jar asked, “I gave you only one jar. Why are you giving me two jars?” The borrower replied, “Your jar gave birth to a little one. I have returned that to you as well.” The fool accepted this and took both jars.

Ten days later the neighbour came and asked to borrow a large, valuable silver jar. Greedy at the thought that this one too might give birth, the man lent him the silver jar as well. Many days passed, but the jar did not come back. After waiting and waiting, the man who had lent the jar—

<!-- anthology scan: 7; printed page: 6; upper shared span -->

—asked the man who had taken it to return the jar. He replied, “Your jar died in childbirth.”

What could he do? A man who believed that a jar could give birth had to believe that it could die as well.

Therefore, one should not behave as though one is honest in small matters and then behave dishonestly in large matters.
