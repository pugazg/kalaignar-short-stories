There were two householders living next door to each other. One was somewhat clever... If one of them was clever, as for the other... let us stop with that. One day this clever man asked his neighbour, “Guests are coming to my house. I do not have even a single jar at home to serve them tea. So lend me the porcelain jar in your house.”

Without hesitation, the neighbour lent him the porcelain jar. The man who borrowed it did not return it for two weeks. After two weeks, he took that porcelain jar back together with two more small baby jars. The lender asked, “I gave you only one porcelain jar. Why have you brought two baby jars along with it?”

The borrower replied, “The reason I did not bring back the porcelain jar you gave me for two weeks is that it became pregnant and gave birth to these two little porcelain jars.” “Is that so!” the other man said, and eagerly accepted them as well.

A month later, the same man—the one who had borrowed the jar—went again to the house of the man who had lent

it and asked, “Guests are coming to my house; give me a jar.” The lender was greedy. If he gave a porcelain jar, it would only give birth to porcelain babies. So, thinking, “Let us give a silver jar; it will give birth to silver babies” (Laughter), he gave him a silver jar. The borrower took it away and then never appeared again. A month or two passed. One day, seeing him on the road, the lender asked, “What happened to the silver jar?” He replied, “It is pregnant.” A few days later, he himself came back. “You said it was pregnant. What happened?” the lender asked. The clever man replied, “The jar died in childbirth.” (Loud laughter)

At once the other man retorted, “How can a jar become pregnant? How can it give birth? Hey! Whom are you trying to fool?”

When there was profit, he did not feel like asking questions. Only when there was a loss did he feel like asking, “Why? How?” This has become a feature of human nature.
