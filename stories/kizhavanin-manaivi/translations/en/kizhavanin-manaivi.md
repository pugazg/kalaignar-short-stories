# The Old Man's Wife

A coil of matted hair on Shiva’s head shook rapidly. Following that movement, Ganga walked along.

<!-- source scan 48; printed page 47 -->

Placing her cotton-soft, catlike feet very lightly on Shiva’s head, she moved somewhere while looking all around.

She went and stood beneath one of the locks of hair, stretching her golden arms among the branches as though waiting for someone. Two long arms reached out from between the branches and covered her blue eyes.

Without her realizing it, Ganga’s hands caught hold of those hands. That joining of hands looked like a rose fixed onto a lily stalk. “Chandra!” The call sounded like the low-register hum of a bee exhausted in the intoxication of drinking honey.

Chandra... yes. Chandra himself. That young man who lives on Shiva’s head. They say cotton and fire should not be kept together. But is not Parameswaran the one who keeps Murugan’s peacock together with a snake? A man who keeps Ganga on his head might at least have kept Chandra at his feet. What can one say? Poor man—such is the writing on his head, his fate.

“My dear Ganga!”

“Chandra, embodiment of dalliance!”

“My love... why did you not come yesterday?”

“Yesterday was my turn. Today is Parvati’s turn.”

“Speak softly... Shiva may wake up.”

“Good thing there is no eye on his head. Even if he wakes, there is nothing to fear.”

“You are a tease, Ganga!”

“And what are you, then?”

“Ganga... do you like talking to that old man?”

“If I liked it... why would I be looking for Chandra?”

“You speak in the same tune as Tara... she is just like this too. They say her husband, with his beard and moustache, torments her in his advanced age.”

“If women like Tara and Ganga end up with old men in their declining years, then it is a hunting ground for Chandras like you.”

The End
