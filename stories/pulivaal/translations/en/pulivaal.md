# The Tiger's Tail

The Malayalam language has not only literature but also sharp, mocking jibes. Malayalam is full of taunting expressions that can sting with a quick “suruk.” During the time I was close to my dear friend M.G.R.—you know that I was close to him for a long time—when we were young I would ask him, “What is the finest, sharply piercing expression of mockery in Malayalam?” When I asked him that, he said, “Nair pudicha

pulivaal.” There is said to be a Tamil proverb corresponding to it. We say, “I don't want the monitor lizard; it is enough if it lets go of my hand.” That is, a man went to catch a monitor lizard. The monitor lizard caught his hand. He did not want the monitor lizard anymore; he begged that it would be enough if it simply let go of his hand. That is how we say it in Tamil.

But “Nair pudicha pulivaal” also gives us a good story. A Nair catches hold of a tiger's tail. The tiger keeps running because it is afraid of the Nair. The Nair keeps holding on to the tiger's tail. The difficulty is this: if the Nair lets go of the tiger's tail, the tiger will leap on him. Because the Nair does not let go, the tiger keeps running. Look at what situations this “tiger's tail caught by the Nair” can fit. (Applause) Whether or not a Nair ever caught a tiger's tail, our country has caught hold of a tiger's tail and is suffering today. (Applause)
