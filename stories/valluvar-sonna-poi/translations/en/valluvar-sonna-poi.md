# Valluvar Sonna Poi

<!-- source scan 4; printed page 3 -->

A hunter comes racing after a deer. A beautiful spotted fawn. Instead of bounding about in the garden where it ought to be running, it is chased by the hunter, who wants to kill it; after darting all over the place, it finally enters Valluvar's house. Valluvar, sitting at the entrance and composing a *kural*, sees the fawn pass him and go into his own house. Ten minutes later the hunter comes running there. Looking at Valluvar, he asks, “Did a fawn come this way?”

What ought Valluvar, who holds that telling a lie is wrong, to have said? “Yes. It came. It is inside the house,” he should have said. He did not. If he had said so, the hunter would have gone inside, dragged that fawn away and eaten it. So he says, ‘It did not come.’ Thinking, “Would Valluvar ever tell a lie?” the hunter goes off in another direction. “Great one! You, who never tell a lie—what is the reason you told a lie like this?”

<!-- source scan 5; printed page 4 -->

Only then does Valluvar say: “It is not a matter of whether what I said was a lie or the truth. Examine whether it was *vaaymai*—truthful speech—or not!! If one says something that causes no harm to others, that is called *vaaymai*. Therefore, even the lie I told will be placed in the place of that *vaaymai*.”
