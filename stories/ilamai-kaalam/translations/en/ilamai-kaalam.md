Even Arjuna did not wage a battle equal to the one Abhimanyu fought in the Bharata war.

For, leaving his very young wife at home, taking leave of her, taking leave of his mother, while his father was fighting on another battlefield, and without even a chance to see his father

either, he entered the battlefield. Even after losing a hand in battle, he gripped a sword with his tongue—with his mouth—and fought to such an extent that he made his enemies turn their backs and flee. At last, the young warrior Abhimanyu died after being stabbed from behind by a traitor named Jeyaprathan. It was in the very prime of his youth that he accomplished that wonder.

Likewise, if we take the Ramayana, Indrajit fought with a daring that even Ravana, even Kumbhakarna, or many other kinds of heroes could not display. He fought to such an extent that Rama, Lakshmana, Hanuman, Sugriva and all the others were thrown into confusion and left unable to understand what was happening; he chased them again and again until, in the end, he died. He too was a young man. He too displayed the surge of his youth on the battlefield, making the day on which Indrajit fought in the Ramayana war a day of fame—just as the day Abhimanyu fought in the Bharata war became famous.

Abhimanyu’s father Arjuna was a renowned archer. He did not earn the fame of being Gandeevan after the age of fifty. He won that fame while he was still a student under Drona. At that time, when the Pandavas and Kauravas were students of Drona, after their training was complete Drona called them together for a test. He showed them a large tree and a parrot sitting on one of its branches, told each of them to bend the bow, and as each bent the bow

he looked at Duryan standing there and asked, “What do you see?” Duryan went on describing: “I see the tree, I see the leaves, I see the branch.” Saying, “You are not the one,” Drona then asked all the warriors. Each said, “I see the tree, I see the branch, I see the leaves.” Setting them all aside, Drona finally called Arjuna and asked, “You look. What do you see?” Arjuna is said to have replied, “I see only the tip of the arrow fixed to my bow and the neck of the parrot sitting on the tree branch.”

At once he released the arrow. The parrot’s neck fell to the ground.
