# Is Cash Easy?

Weary from touring his constituency, Lanjayya dozed for a while in the reclining chair at the front of his house. By some fluke he had managed to get the people’s votes; and when he and his opponent ended up exactly tied, he became the representative of the constituency through the drawing of a lot. He had no occupation of his own. Not an inch of land had been left to him by grandfather, great-grandfather, mother or father. He knew no trade. Yet within a year or two of winning the election, a large house and ten acres of land had become his. Do not ask how. It was the gain he had earned through an occupation suited to his name. Surely it was so that he might become such a great man that God Himself had chosen him through that lot and made him labour every day for the people. Ah! What labour. What labour! Even that day, having gone around the constituency from morning till evening, he had grown exhausted because he had not received wages commensurate with his labour, and had collapsed into the chair. Sleep came and went. Since none of the things he had expected had reached his hands, sleep was playing hide-and-seek with him.

“We recommended a loan from the Collector’s office for fertilizer and draught bullocks for that Sevathan. What was due has not come. That fellow is nowhere

to be seen. For that Dr. Siranjeevi we climbed all seven steps and got him a ‘transfer.’ He said he would take care of us; for four days he has not shown his face. The face I woke up to yesterday was thoroughly bad luck. If some fellow with a heavy purse comes for a recommendation, then our fortunes are made! If only dried-up wretches come, what can we do? We have to milk them as though milking a wooden cow. Damn! Not even that cursed thing has turned up today!”

Waves of thought kept stirring his eyelids and would not let him sleep. With his eyes lightly closed, in a state somewhere between wakefulness and sleep, Mr. Lanjayya was carried, without even realizing it, into a series of imaginary events beyond his control.

Even when he was a baby, the old women next door still jokingly say, he would drink his mother’s milk only if a quarter-anna coin was pressed into his palm. Whether or not that is true, his trusted friends swear that when he was a student he would say, “This is the question that will come in the exam!” and sell each question for one rupee. Whether those questions actually appeared was another matter!

What would a man of such talent be like when he travelled in the chariot of imagination?

Britain, Japan, France and other countries. The people of those lands all stare up at the sky, waiting for the aeroplane to land. From the aircraft itself, Lanjayya speaks through a loudspeaker. The people of each country hear him.

“Oh... my friends! If my foot is to touch your country, that is no ordinary matter. You must grant agency rights to the companies run by my family. You must give every assistance for my sons’ businesses to grow. Only if you promise that will I set foot in your airport. What do you say? In appreciation of my virtue in never misusing office, will you honour my recommendations?”

On hearing this dignified speech, everyone applauds. Luxury goods from foreign lands are hurled toward the flying aeroplane. Agency approvals come hurtling through the air. After that Lanjayya gets down from the plane. Alas, poor man! While the aircraft is still in the sky, he opens the door and steps out....... He falls; he falls headfirst; he rolls his eyes and looks around... Before him is a flood of people. In its midst stands a decorated platform. On that platform is a stout, dark, imposing man. “Democracy is my life—my breath—my speech,” he declares with heroic fervour.

Right there, with the aid of certain mechanical devices, a great studio proprietor turns that speech into gramophone records and keeps producing them as though flipping dosas on a griddle. A scream of “Veeer!” is heard from above, and everyone lifts their head to look. “Alas! Has this fate befallen my dearest friend?” cries the socialist orator. In anguish he leaps and, just before Lanjayya can hit the ground, scoops him up bodily. At the sight of that extraordinary ‘stunt’ scene, the human flood roars, “Long live democratic socialism!” The tremendous sound shakes the platform, makes the earth tremble, carves several cracks in the sky and knocks down seven or eight stars—not cinema stars! They had already fallen upon that stage. Amid that democratic-socialist roar rising like the deluge at the end of the world, the Ganga and the Kaveri overflow their banks and become one. Which is the Indian Ocean? Which is the Arabian Sea? Which is the Bay of Bengal? No difference can be seen. One vast wilderness of water! Trees, brushwood, grass, plants, living creatures—nothing at all is visible above it. Only fish may still be there beneath the water. Above the surface floats a single banyan leaf. On that leaf lies a thin, long figure; hollow cheeks; a white cap on his head; only his mouth keeps muttering, “I created this cosmic flood in order to abolish corruption.” Then a large vaalai fish comes toward the banyan leaf. only the head of Mr. Lanjayya! The rest of its body and its tail are just like a fish. “Gulp!” The banyan leaf and the man muttering upon it disappear together into the vaalai fish’s belly. The instant the leaf and the man are swallowed, a great change comes over the world! Everything returns to how it was before.

Mr. Lanjayya is now standing at the entrance of a general hospital in some city. Why? Is some relative of his ill? Nothing of the sort; he is the one who must open the gate and grant permission to those coming in and going out. And the people coming in? From outside the gate they plead with Mr. Lanjayya for something. Lanjayya snarls at them. Then they put their hands into their shirt pockets, untie knots in the ends of their garments, and give him one thing or another. While taking these things, Lanjayya’s eyes circle around him. Then the gate opens. The relatives of patients fighting for life inside, delighted at having paid the entrance fee, run toward the designated ‘wards.’ There are many good faces there, serving as doctors. And then there is one face...... yes, the very same face....... The face that had been standing at the hospital entrance is now moving about as the chief doctor of the maternity ward! Before Dr. Lanjayya stands a full-term pregnant woman, her eyes all sores, pleading, “Sir! Sir! I cannot bear this pain

any longer, sir! Somehow admit me right now, sir!”

“Chi! Nonsense! Get out!” Dr. Lanjayya snaps and walks away. Without letting the momentum of that anger subside, he gives the newborn baby the injection that was meant for the mother. Just then some ‘patient’ holds out a ten-rupee note. In his eagerness to take it, he does not even notice that the baby has breathed its last. He lights a cigarette and comes out into the outer corridor.

There, the full-term pregnant woman who had first been begging for admission lies flat on her back on the floor. The sight fills Dr. Lanjayya with irritation. “Worthless dogs! Why do creatures like these need children at all? She cannot give me ten rupees as ‘pocket money’ for admission, but she wants a government hospital for childbirth? On a fine tarred road, why not spread some straw and give birth there!” he curses inwardly. Then he shouts angrily at a man standing there, “Hey! Who is there? Why is this woman lying here? Get her up and drive her away.”

“You cannot wake her up, you sinner! She has gone to Yama’s world!” the man replies, and leaps upon the doctor like a tiger. Blow after blow lands on Dr. Lanjayya’s face—“Thomeel! Thomeel!”

Lanjayya collapses... “Supreme sinner! Criminal! Outcaste! In a government charity hospital, are there fat rats like you too? How many mothers have you killed like this, together with the children in their wombs? Can hearts as rotten as yours enter this sacred profession? Sinner!” screams the dead woman’s husband, stamping again and again on Dr. Lanjayya as he lies on the ground. The ‘money purse’ bulges out of the doctor’s shirt pocket, and the ten-rupee notes inside fly into the air.

As he is being trampled, Dr. Lanjayya vanishes as if by magic. “Hey! I have arrested you!” comes an authoritative voice. The pregnant woman’s husband, suddenly brought back to his senses, turns to look. Mr. Lanjayya, now in the form of a sub-inspector, threatens him with a handgun... The ten-rupee notes still flying in the air are gathered together by his hands and, without anyone seeing, stuffed into his trouser pocket. With those same hands he lands a blow on the pregnant woman’s husband’s neck. The man has made up his mind to die. What is left for him in life now? She too is gone—mercifully she went without bringing out what was still in her womb. So there is no obstacle left between him and death. He speaks boldly.

“I have seen so many police officers as friends of the public—as companions of the poor—as embodiments of honesty—as shadows of justice—

is there a creature like this in that same rank?” he asks, anger overflowing, firing the question like an arrow.

Inspector Lanjayya gives him a very gentle reply, with great humility. “Those are all police officers who do not know how to make a living. I am one who strictly follows the old saying, ‘Winnow while there is wind.’ You can escape too. If you have fifty, take it out!”

At once comes the shock of an earthquake—the fiery lava of a volcano scatters in all directions—a storm wind spews poison—the hospital buildings become dust! Dust! A single dance of fire! The whole town burns! The whole world burns! After a short while, silence.

A rocket travels toward the moon. The people of the lunar world wait eagerly, wondering what new object is arriving. Mr. Lanjayya gets down from the ‘rocket.’ Those people surround him and welcome him. Mr. Lanjayya makes a triumphant tour of the whole lunar world. Countless gifts pile up. Gold and diamonds are given in sack after sack. “Take all these away and spend them to make the world below prosperous!” the people of the moon say at the farewell ceremony. A foolish grin spreads across Mr. Lanjayya’s face. “The world below? These will not even be enough for the expenses of my children, little ones and relatives!” he laments.

The rocket returns toward Earth. Those who had struggled so hard to send him off surround him, honour him with garlands, and eagerly ask about the wonders of the moon. “Do you think telling you those wonders is such an ordinary matter? You must give me fifty thousand rupees,” he declares categorically in a statement. Lying in the reclining chair at the front of his house, checking the newspapers to see whether the statement he issued has appeared without a single letter changed, he sinks into a half-sleep in which he both knows and does not know himself.

At that moment Yama Dharma comes racing in a ‘Government Transport bus.’ The instant the bus reaches the entrance of Mr. Lanjayya’s house, Yama shouts “Stop!” and, before the bus itself has fully stopped, jumps down and enters the front part of Lanjayya’s house. Mr. Lanjayya realizes in an instant that something shocking has happened and snaps, “Who is that?” The following conversation then takes place.

“It is I—Yama Dharma!”

“Where is your buffalo vehicle?”

“I have lent it to government departments.”

“What happened to their tortoise vehicle?”

“It is under repair!”

“The snail vehicle too?”

“They have already sent that one to run the municipalities!”

“Oh! So the departments keep the buffalo and the snail, and only for crashing into people on the streets do they keep deer vehicles and rabbit vehicles?”

“Leave that aside. Come, let us go!”

“Where?”

“Still do not understand? You are dead! Come, let us go to Yama’s world.”

“Yama’s world? Are there Panchayat Unions, municipalities, corporations, legislative assemblies, upper houses, Parliament, Rajya Sabha and such things there? Are there parties? Are there elections?”

“Not one damned thing. It is one peaceful world.”

“Chi! Chi! I do not like peaceful places! For my business to work, there must be plenty of uproar and commotion. So I cannot come with you.”

“Alas, Mr. Lanjayya! You must not say that. Otherwise I will acquire the disgrace of having failed in my duty.”

“Hey, Yama Dharma! Do you know the one way to keep that disgrace from coming upon you? If I come with you, who will protect my children and little ones? Somehow, I, a man with no occupation,

who knows no trade, have struggled mightily through the drawing of a lot and accumulated this house worth one lakh rupees and this land worth fifty thousand. Will that be enough for my descendants? So I have one condition. If I am to come with you, you must give me a cheque for five lakh rupees right now.”

Yama Dharma’s head spins on hearing this. “A bribe even to die?” he mutters.

“Please, do not take another birth. Ask for that one now as well. Give me a little ‘concession’!”

“All right; make it ten lakhs.”

Immediately Yama uses the ‘telephone’ there to call Kubera Bank and asks for information. The reply comes that only eight lakhs stand in Yama’s name. Yama thinks deeply. A decision occurs to him. He understands that this man must be struck dead with money itself. He takes out a cheque and writes something on it. Then he holds out the cheque and moves toward Mr. Lanjayya, saying:

“Here! Cash—easy? By cash; you; die!”

Mr. Lanjayya looks at the cheque in his hand: fifty lakhs.

“Ah! Fifty lakhs!” Mr. Lanjayya’s mouth falls open; after that he never closes it. Yama has tricked him and taken him away.

“What is this? I told you that contractor said he would come and see you today. Did he come?” asks Mr. Lanjayya’s wife eagerly as she enters. Seeing her husband’s head hanging limp, she faints and falls to the floor.
