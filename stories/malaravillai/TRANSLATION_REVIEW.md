## Structural / rhetorical review

- Aadhavan's opening ethical position criticises exploitative employers and dutyless workers alike, as in the Tamil source;
- the repeated transformations of the studio set contrast with the unchanging loft, light and labour;
- the studio commands and cameraman types preserve the worker's vantage point on cinema production;
- Aadhavan's rationalist mockery of equipment worship and his sympathy for producers coexist without translator-added ideological explanation;
- his failed acting ambition and devotion to his mother lead directly into the marriage-savings thread;
- the Anandhi memory is allowed to move from childhood innocence to adolescent romantic expectation in the source's order;
- the new film *Malarnthathu* (“bloomed” in the Tamil thematic field) becomes the setting in which Aadhavan's own hoped-for life does not bloom; the English does not explain this inside the narrative;
- Anandhi's transformation into Asha is revealed first visually to Aadhavan, then confirmed by the co-worker, preserving the source's timing;
- Asha's failure to recognise the dead Aadhavan and her later anger about the lost contract remain as stark as the source presents them;
- the mother's marriage savings becoming funeral expenses is preserved as the final reversal;
- no outside film-history, social, linguistic or political commentary was inserted into the English story body.
