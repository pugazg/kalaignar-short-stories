# Nariyappan of Nandiyur

Dear Nariyappar,

Greetings from Nandiyappan. I trust all the friends in Nandiyur are well. I pray to Chamundeshwari that the people of Nandiyur win a majority of places in the coming contest as well. Even so, my people of Nariyur are not without grievance against the people of Nandiyur. I was worshipped as their second god, worthy of their love and respect. As the one who turned Nariyur into a town of light and won for that town a unique fame throughout the world, my townspeople have shown me extraordinary devotion. They continue to do so even now. You surely know that one of the peerless groves in the world is in Nariyur, and that I am the owner of a graceful mansion that sheds light! I have many times told you with tears in my eyes that I remain forever indebted to the guileless love and affection of my townspeople. The whole world knows that even

today my townspeople consider it their good fortune to worship me by placing me in a most exalted, principled, high-status, state-honoured position.

What can be done? Would the national awakening that arose as a result of the storm of liberation suited to the times, and the breeze of freedom that began to blow after it, leave only our Nariyur untouched? Had the conditions of those days been in the hands of those who are in power now, perhaps the lavish state dignities that belonged to me would not have been taken away. But the Iron Man of those days paid no heed to any of that. Before he could so much as move his eyes, I understood the situation and surrendered everything that had to be surrendered. Perhaps so that my heart would not be wounded all at once, they let me remain in my own place and, through that, gave the people of Nariyur who were devoted to me some consolation for a few years. But then the pressure that came upon them compelled them to tell me to move my residence from Nariyur and come to your Nandiyur.

Had it been only a transfer of residence, I would not have felt so much difficulty in accepting it. It seems I am also to be the person who gives assent to, and supervises, the administration conducted by Nandiyur's administrators. I have heard that the people of Nandiyur themselves do not want this post, and that they hate it as an unnecessary post, like a goat's beard; and I

heard all this. You know how great a moral predicament I myself would consider it to take up that post. My story has become like having a gold-capped walking stick taken away and a rough staff put into my hand! Would that rough staff at least help beat a snake? Certainly not. What work is there for the post imposed on me in your Nandiyur? What duty is there to perform? It is nothing but the work of an “assenter”!

The administrators have their clerks write something down; I must stand up and read it before the prominent people of the town. Many of those prominent people then criticize what is good and bad in what I have read. In our Nariyur no one ever criticized my views. That is one pain in my present condition: they analyze my views and express approval and disapproval. The second pain is this: are the views being criticized even my views? They are not. They are the administrators' own views! I am being used as a shield to absorb the arrows aimed in opposition to the mistakes they make.

Was I ever accustomed to such a life? From the moment I rise at dawn: varied amusements—cheerful recreation—a motionless, tranquil life—thought of the Lord—on such a charming foundation, from childhood

<!-- anthology scan: 52; printed page: 50 -->

I have grown up. When the people of Nariyur are waiting to proclaim that whatever I say is scripture, why do I need a post that makes me the protective shield of Nandiyur's administrators?

You may say, “Does it not bring in many lakhs?” Am I a man who has never seen lakhs? By the greatness of Mahishasuri, rubies, vaiduryam stones, emeralds, gomethakam stones, gold, diamonds, green gems and coral are set not only in the legs of the silken couch on which I lie and roll, but even in the paths trodden by my feet in my palace!

Some circumstance or other has forced me to agree to this “assenter” post. Even as I accepted it, my thought was this... They say King Vikramaditya ruled six months in the land and six months in the forest; likewise, I decided that if I sit in this assenter's post for one week, I will seat someone else in it for nine weeks and set off travelling. When I thought about whom else to seat there, I thought of you. There is, after all, a custom in Nandiyur that another person qualified to sit in the assenter's post is the local panchayat man, is there not? In accordance with that custom, I have decided to give you that opportunity. Whenever the assenter encounters an unavoidable obstruction, suffers ill health, or even passes

<!-- anthology scan: 53; printed page: 51 -->

away, it is customary in such difficult circumstances for the panchayat man to sit in that post for a few days. But I am going to change that custom. I will make it so that the townspeople call you the assenter, while I preserve my old title so that the people do not forget it. I firmly believe that you will not refuse this. No one can find fault with me in this matter. If the Nandiyur administrators do not like this “Vikramaditya method,” they can sensibly abolish the assenter's post altogether and earn a good name. If they do not wish to do that, I swear: let them understand that I am not the man to read out before the townspeople whatever those administrators write and hand me. If this decision of mine gives you boundless delight, I shall smile.

Affectionately,

Nandiyappan,
