# Kuruvi Rameswaram

<!-- source scan 5; printed page 4 -->

There is an orally handed-down tale in the Ramayana. When Rama was wandering in search of Sita, he is said to have come near our Tiruvarur.

When he came there, a sparrow bowed to him and asked, “I want a boon.” “What boon?” Rama

<!-- source scan 6; printed page 5 -->

asked. “I want to go to Rameswaram,” the sparrow is said to have asked as its boon.

The sparrow's wings were very small. “Rameswaram is a place far away. You cannot fly such a long distance. So, little sparrow, for your sake I shall create a Rameswaram right here,” Rama is said to have told it. With the tip of his arrow he drew a line there, made a small pond, and said, “If you bathe in this, you will receive merit.”

The story says that the sparrow listened to Rama, bathed in the pond made there by his arrow, and gained merit. Even now that place is called Kuruvi Rameswaram.

“..... For me, unable to go to Rameswaram, you have shown a Rameswaram right here; likewise, even though I could not go to the World Tamil Conference—because the self-respect sentiments by which I could fittingly go there had been shut out—you have created a World Tamil Conference here for me, just as Rama created a Rameswaram for the sparrow,” you said, on behalf of your hearts overflowing with affection, calling this a World Tamil Conference.
