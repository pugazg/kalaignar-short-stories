A good snake is said to have gone to a sage and complained. “Everyone is afraid when they see me. My name is ‘good snake.’ Yet people tremble when they see me. Why?” it asked. The sage said, “You bite everyone. It is because you bite them that, even though your name is ‘good snake,’

people are afraid of you. So from now on, do not bite anyone,” the sage advised. The snake went away. A few months later, the snake came to see the sage. Its whole body was covered in wounds. “Why have you come back so badly wounded?” the sage asked. The snake said, “As you told me, I did not bite anyone. So everyone who saw me beat me again and again. These are the wounds from that,” and shed tears. The sage is said to have replied: “I told you not to bite. Did I say you should not even give a hiss? Do not bite. But if, when people came to beat you, you had hissed at them once, they would not have beaten you, would they? Forget biting. But do not forget to hiss.”

In the same way, one may be good. But one must not become cowardly. One may be gentle; one must not become utterly meek. A heart that surges at the sight of evil is certainly necessary.
