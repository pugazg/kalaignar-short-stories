# The Invalid in the Rich Man's House

“Alas! If that life is lost, I’ll go mad. My wife already has a heart condition! If she suffers a shock like this, her heart will stop with a ‘posak.’ My daughter is studying at ‘Queen Mary’s’; how am I to console her? The moment she hears the news she’ll come running. This year’s studies—exams—everything will be ruined! Ruined! Ruined, that’s all!”

—Saying this aloud, the rich man Sengamalam Pillai burst into a ‘kor’-like wail and poured out tears.

His wife Maragatham stood motionless, like a tree. Just as raindrops resting on leaves fall ‘drip, drip’ when the wind stirs them, water kept pouring from her eyes.

The servant Ponnan’s legs would not move; nor would his hands. He went around telling all the neighbours about the turmoil in the rich man’s house and returned with a great crowd.

People from the street, townspeople, and important local figures all hurried to Sengamalam Pillai’s house.

Seeing the grief on Sengamalam Pillai’s face, they could not bring themselves to speak to him. They looked at one another, sat without a sound, and watched what was happening.

The “telephone” rang. Ponnan ran to answer it.

“Hello! Hello! What?... A ‘trunk call’?—,from Madras?... Just a moment!”

“Master! Master! From the city... a ‘trunk call’...”

Sengamalam Pillai slowly got up, went over, and began to speak.

“Hello!...Hello!...This is Sengamalam Pillai speaking...Oh! Lalitha?...What is it, dear?...It’s still the same!...A little ‘serious,’ that’s all! I told you this morning; the ‘condition’ is still the same now; the doctors say there’s nothing to be afraid of! Don’t worry needlessly, dear!...Keeping that life alive is our responsibility—my dear child...why are you crying like a little girl?...Oh dear...what...crying over the phone itself?...(weeping himself) God won’t abandon us, dear!...Don’t worry...I’ll keep telling you the condition once every hour. You two are like my two eyes!...Would I ever agree to lose one of my eyes?...Don’t cry, Lalitha!...‘Extension please’...Once the danger stage is past, I’ll send you good news tomorrow itself. Everything will turn out well.

Next month we can celebrate the wedding happily...When the astrologer has said that your wedding will definitely take place next month...how can news of mourning possibly arise in our house before then?...Don’t be afraid...! All right...I’ll talk later. ‘Phone’ again after another hour! Yes! Yes! Mm—hang up!”

Having consoled his daughter in Madras, Sengamalam Pillai stood longing for the doctor’s arrival, with no one capable of consoling him.

Of the two ‘cars’ that had gone to fetch the doctor, the doctor got out of one. From the other ‘car’ he took the ‘sethascope’ that had been riding there, put it on, and hurried inside. This was the fifth time since morning that the doctor had come there! Even so, Sengamalam Pillai was not satisfied!

“Doctor sir! You must stay right here; we’re terribly frightened!” the rich man pleaded.

“Don’t be afraid!...I’m coming ten times a day as it is; there’s a ‘nurse’ right nearby too...what is there to fear?” he said reassuringly as he went in.

The nurse inside the room greeted the doctor and led him in. Sengamalam Pillai tried to follow the doctor. But the nurse stopped Pillai from coming

inside, flashing him a smile that said he should not.

When would the doctor come out? What news would he give?—Sengamalam Pillai, Maragatham, and the others stood with anxious expectation gathered on their faces. No information came from inside. Only silence; deep silence!

Suddenly the nurse’s loud, tinkling laughter echoed outside. ‘Could that perhaps be crying? Alas, have we been ruined?’ Sengamalam Pillai thought for a moment, then reassured himself.

“My heart is burning like fire! What is there to laugh about now! These doctors are all like this—stone-hearted people!” muttered the distressed rich man as he paced about.

Fifteen minutes later the doctor came out. “It is a little serious! I can say only after five this evening. Until then it is the danger stage! I’ll write down an important injection...Tell them to buy it and keep it ready; if I come at four and give that injection, we may see some improvement. We’ll try the final measures!” The doctor wrote out the medicine, handed it over, and got into his car.

With tears streaming, the rich man saw the doctor off and stood on the verandah staring blankly at the sky.

A great commotion in the street suddenly drew his attention. Four or five people came running toward his house. Hair dishevelled! Among them was a woman of about forty. She cried out to the rich man:

“Alas, master! My daughter’s sari caught fire and her whole body is burnt! She’s writhing like a worm. We have to take her to the hospital. Please make some way for us, sir!”

—The mother fell to the ground and rolled about, weeping.

“Just lend us a car, master! If we take her to the hospital at once, she’ll survive!” cried the girl’s father.

“A car? To hell with the car!......Can’t you see what a man is suffering here?......I’m writhing in agony......Don’t come at the wrong time and take my life too. Go away!” the rich man snapped. He went inside, opened his iron safe, took out a hundred-rupee note, put it in Ponnan’s hand, and ordered, “Take this, go by car to the medical shop, buy the injection the doctor wrote down, and come back quickly!”....

Ponnan left in the car. The poor street family that had come seeking the rich man’s help

went back home, perhaps thinking that with their own tears they might bathe the body of the daughter they had borne and soothe the burning caused by the fire.

Her name was Chelli! Chellamma was the name she had been given—“Chelli” was the shorter, sweeter name by which they called her! She was the lamp that lit that whole house! One could say that those parents kept breath in their bodies only to make her future bloom with floral radiance. A family surviving on daily-wage labour—and into that family, such a great storm!

Chelli’s sari caught fire while she was boiling gruel on the stove. Because her sari had plenty of the dirt and oil that fire needed, the flames spread quickly. Chelli’s body was badly burnt. Unable to stand, unable to sit, unable to lie down—unable to do anything—she ran here and there, screaming and writhing. Only in the lap of death lies the great cooling medicine that can quell such burning! But the poor parents did not want to lay their daughter in that lap. How could they? They writhed as though their own bodies had caught fire. They begged the rich man. No ‘car’ was available. By God’s grace, they put together a load, cut banana stems and spread them on it, and laid Chelli

down on them and began whatever treatment they could manage.

The clock struck four!

As promised, the doctor entered the rich man’s house. The purchased injection was handed to him. The doctor went inside. The nurse, as usual, received him and led him in. The rich man’s family waited with eyes and ears sharpened for whatever news might come from inside. Within ten minutes the doctor came out.

“Go at once and bring Lady Doctor Jansi!” he said, then went back inside.

The sound of groaning continued from within!

“Ah! Alas!......Mother!........”

Whose voice was that?

The rich man listened intently!

It was the nurse’s voice!

Why should she be groaning?

The rich man was lost in astonishment.

Lady Doctor Jansi arrived too.

A few minutes after she entered the room, the doctor came out.

“What is the matter?” the rich man asked anxiously.

The doctor did not answer. He smiled. The groaning grew louder!

The sky darkened! Terrible thunder! Lightning! Heavy rain! A ghostly gale! All within a short while!.......

The groaning inside stopped! Suddenly, a new voice!

“Kuwa! Kuwa!”

Lady Doctor Jansi came out!

“A normal delivery!” she said.

“For whom? What is this?”——the rich man asked with a pounding heart.

“For no one else! For the nurse in this room, of course,” the doctor said with a laugh.

“Thank goodness! Was my house the only place available to deliver the nurse’s baby?......Doctor sir! Please attend to the work you came for!...It’s almost five! Has the danger stage passed or not?”

“Here......we’ll know in a few minutes......At exactly five, consciousness must return, the eyes must open—the mouth must open!”

The doctor spoke confidently. Everyone watched the hands of the clock move with anxious attention.

Dong! Dong!! Dong!!! Dong!!!! Dong!!!!! The clock struck five! ....

The doctor went inside!

Complete silence!

Suddenly, a growling sound!

Then a bark: “Val! Val!”

“Ah! My Chelli has survived!” cried the rich man as he ran inside. The ‘Chelli’ in the rich man’s house had survived!

But...

A funeral bier was passing along the street! On it, Chelli, the labourer’s daughter, was going in procession!
