# Hazrat Ali and the Jew

> **English translation of the verified Tamil archival reading.** Controlling source: 1987 collection `கலைஞர் சொன்ன குட்டிக் கதைகள்`, lower scan **25 / printed page 24 → scan 26 / printed page 25 → upper scan 27 / printed page 26**. The verified Tamil remains authoritative.

<!-- anthology scan: 25; printed page: 24; source span: lower -->

Once, before Hazrat Ali (rali), the Prophet's (sal) son-in-law, cousin and heir, a young man spoke insultingly of Islam, God and the faith. At once Ali (rali) sprang at him, pushed him down, climbed onto his chest and sat there. The Jew became afraid that he was

<!-- anthology scan: 26; printed page: 25 -->

going to be killed. With not the slightest hope of escape, he spat in Ali's (rali) face.

You may have seen how a vessel full of milk, when it boils and rises on the stove, subsides as soon as a little cold water is sprinkled over it. In the same way, as soon as the Jew spat, Ali's (rali) fierce anger astonishingly subsided. Without doing anything to the Jew, he got up and went away. Astonished by this unexpected turn, the Jew ran after Ali (rali), stopped him, and asked: “Your conduct is very strange. For one word I spoke, you knocked me down and even tried to kill me. When, having lost all hope, I spat in your face, you left without doing anything to me. Why?”

“You insulted our God. For that reason I thought of killing you. But when you spat on me, if I became angry it would be for a personal matter of my own. It is not right to combine personal hatred with public service. I may take a life for Allah, but I must not become a killer for Ali,” he replied. Gentlemen, both of us—Shaukat Ali and Muhammad Ali—bear that honorable

<!-- anthology scan: 27; printed page: 26; source span: upper -->

name of Ali. Comparing themselves with that Ali, those Ali brothers said that if our policy is insulted, if the philosophy we have taken into our hearts is insulted, we will dare anything. But even if someone spits in our face personally, what he has insulted is not the policy, not the faith, not God; he has insulted me personally. So even if he spits, never mind—that is what the Ali brothers said then.

That is what I remember now, when I see people spitting at us: what our Anna said, “Let us forget; let us forgive.”

## Translation note

- Source abbreviations `(ஸல்)` and `(ரலி)` are conservatively romanized as `(sal)` and `(rali)` rather than expanded from outside sources.
- Source-sensitive forms and the personal/public-service contrast were translated without altering the Tamil.
