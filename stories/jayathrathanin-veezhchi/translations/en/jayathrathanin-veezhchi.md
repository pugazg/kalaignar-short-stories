# The Fall of Jayadratha

> **English translation of the verified Tamil archival reading.** Controlling source: 1987 collection `கலைஞர் சொன்ன குட்டிக் கதைகள்`, lower scan **34 / printed page 33 → 35 / 34 → 36 / 35 → upper 37 / 36**.

<!-- anthology scan: 34; printed page: 33; source span: lower -->

Abhimanyu falls on the battlefield of the Bharata war.

Jayadratha, fighting as one of the commanders of the Kauravas, kills Abhimanyu from behind. Abhimanyu has broken into the chakravyuha and is fighting within it, at a time when no one can defeat him, and Jayadratha kills him from behind.

The news that Abhimanyu has died reaches Arjuna, who is on another battlefield. Kannan is driving Arjuna's chariot. Struck by the shocking news that his son has died in that day's battle, Arjuna vows: “I will kill the man who killed my son Abhimanyu before the sun sets this evening. If I cannot kill him, I will build up a pit of fire, fall into it and die.”

Kannan listens in silence.

<!-- anthology scan: 35; printed page: 34 -->

All through the day Arjuna searches everywhere for his enemy Jayadratha. He cannot find him. They take Jayadratha away and hide him—because Arjuna can kill him only if he sees him. Arjuna becomes exhausted from searching and searching.

Evening begins to darken. The sun sets. In accordance with his vow, Arjuna decides to build up a pit of fire and fall into it, and he walks around the fire pit. He has gone around it twice.

Meanwhile, news reaches the Kauravas that Arjuna is about to fall into the fire and die.

Jayadratha has a desire! He wants to watch Arjuna fall into the fire and die. The very Jayadratha who killed Abhimanyu develops such a perverse desire.

They seat him in a howdah on an elephant, bring him to the crest of a hill and place him there, saying, “Watch that pleasing sight.”

He sits there watching Arjuna go around the fire for the third time, about to fall into it.

<!-- anthology scan: 36; printed page: 35 -->

Then Kannan, who has been coming behind Arjuna, asks:

“Partha, what do you want at the very end?” Arjuna replies:

“Paramatma, I need nothing else. I want a little sunlight.”

Then Kannan says, “The sun has not really set, my boy! I have hidden it with my discus weapon. Deceived by that, Jayadratha has come and seated himself over there. Look! I am removing the discus; the sun will appear.” When Kannan removes the discus, the sun shines. At that very instant, as the sunlight falls, Arjuna strings his bow and releases an arrow, bringing down Jayadratha's head. Thus that scene in the Bharata war ends.

It is just the same in the Legislative Assembly: the DMK has been defeated, like Abhimanyu dying! M.G.R. is seated like Jayadratha in a howdah on an elephant, wanting to see the DMK suffer defeat after defeat and be destroyed.

We have neither Kannan with us nor our beloved Anna. Yet although the people of Tamil Nadu had hidden the sunlight for two or three years, they now look at us and say: “You want light, do you not? Here is the sun—there is Jayadratha,”

<!-- anthology scan: 37; printed page: 36; source span: upper -->

and we take up the bow! We set the arrow to the bow! The eye sees! The people of the country see the DMK not falling into the fire!

Certainly neither the DMK nor its allied parties will fall into the fire.

It is the authoritarian forces—the anti-worker forces—these feelings that will fall into the fire; neither the DMK nor the allied parties that support it will become prey to that fire!
