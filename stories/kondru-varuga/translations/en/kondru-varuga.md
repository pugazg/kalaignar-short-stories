# Go and Kill Him!

Kovalan comes to the market street to sell the anklet of Kannagi, embodiment of chastity. There he shows it to an elder.

<!-- source scan 9; printed page 33 -->

“Ah, son!... This is an anklet beyond price. Even if I sold all I own, I would not have enough to buy it. Take it to the palace goldsmith!... He will sell it for you,” says the elder. Then, looking down the street, he says, “Well, well... there he is now. Goldsmith! Goldsmith! Stop!” The elder who said this stops the chief goldsmith. The hundred goldsmiths who came with him stop as well. Then the chief goldsmith looks at the elder who called him and asks, “What...... what?”

“This man has a beautiful anklet,” says the elder.

“An anklet?”.....

“Yes. A priceless anklet!... He wants it sold.” Then, looking at Kovalan, the elder says, “Give it to him, son.”

Kovalan too, to hand his anklet to the chief goldsmith—

“Now my work is done. I am leaving,” says the elder as he goes away.

The goldsmith walks in silence, looking at the anklet. Kovalan walks behind him. The hundred goldsmiths follow. For some reason, the goldsmiths keep looking at one another.

When they reach a large hall, the goldsmith stops and asks Kovalan, “What town are you from?”

“Poompuhar...”

“Name?...”

“I have fallen so low as to sell my wife’s anklet... I do not wish to tell you my name. Forgive me....”

“How did you come to me to sell the anklet?”

Not understanding what lies behind the question, Kovalan stares at him.

“Never mind. We must thank that elder. Everything is for the good! The Pandya queen needs a single anklet. The peerless anklet she desires—this can only be it. What I searched for has come to me of itself. Stay in this hall with the anklet. I will inform the king and come back to take you with me,” says the chief goldsmith.

“Many thanks,” says Kovalan. Looking at the hundred men, the chief goldsmith says, “You too remain here as company for him. I will be back soon.” With that, he leaves.

* * *

The bedchamber of Nedunchezhiyan, vanquisher of the Aryan army.

The Pandyan king, dressed and ready to leave for the assembly, looks at the queen and asks, “It is getting late!... Are you not coming to court?”

“I am not coming with a foot that has no anklet!”

“Today in court I am going to find the anklet thief too!....”

“Ah!... What concern! You have time to write *purappattu*, gather war matériel, and spend all day hearing news of the Pandya realm, but do you have any concern that your consort’s foot lies here stripped of its radiance?....”

“Do not forget that the country is greater than you and me! Should a queen worry so much over a paltry anklet?”

“To you it may be a paltry anklet! I myself have become paltry to you, haven’t I? That is why...”

“All right...all right...are you coming to court or not?”

“Without the anklet, I will not take even one step from here.”

“Mm...all right...I am going,” says Pandyan Nedunchezhiyan, and he strides away. Outside, the officer—the anklet thief—is waiting. On seeing the king, he bows and follows him. When Nedunchezhiyan reaches the corridor, the chief goldsmith bows his head before him.

“What?....”

“The thief who stole the anklet has been caught, my king?....”

“Where is he?”

The officer who actually stole the anklet starts in alarm.

“He was trying to repair the anklet and sell it. I caught him and—

<!-- source scan 10; printed page 34 -->

—secured him and ran here with the news,” says the goldsmith.

“Is that so?” Nedunchezhiyan turns to the officer. “A thief in Pandya country? Go and kill him!”

Kovalan is pacing about in the temple hall. Near a platform resembling a sacrificial altar, the hundred goldsmiths are seated, all looking at him. In the distance the chief goldsmith and the officer approach. The goldsmiths rise when they see them.

“Goldsmith! Did your errand succeed?” Kovalan asks eagerly.

“My errand succeeded! Yours has ended in failure!” says the goldsmith.

“Then the king does not need the anklet?”

“Why would the king need an anklet?... It is the queen who needs one...”

“Goldsmith!......” Kovalan is angered by the mockery.

“Give him the anklet,” says the goldsmith, pointing to the officer.

The officer stretches out his hand, and Kovalan gives him the anklet.

“How did you come by this anklet?” asks the officer.

“It is my wife’s anklet...”

“Why are you selling it...”

“A buyer does not need to ask that...”

“Hmm... You are selling a single anklet! At least explain that!....”

“I will name a price for the anklet; I will not explain why I am selling it!”

“A skilled thief!”

“What?” Kovalan is stunned.

“Yes. Otherwise, as though the flesh had been swallowed while the skin remained, with the locked chest still locked and the key still with me, how could this anklet have been stolen...?” says the chief goldsmith.

“Ah!... Did I steal the anklet? Goldsmith!... Why this accusation against me? What injustice is this?” Kovalan asks, trembling.

“And stealing the queen’s anklet alone was just?....”

“Who stole your queen’s anklet?.... You who have no way to find it—no ability... are you putting the blame on a man passing by?....”

The officer springs up. “Hold your tongue!... In the Pandya realm, where even the shadow of the five great sins cannot enter, a sin has entered in your form. On hearing it, the king shudders... trembles!....”

“I will see that king... I am no thief, officer! Because I lost my senses, I left the wife whose hand I took afloat in tears; I destroyed mountains of gold... mountains of diamonds... mountains of silver beyond counting... destroyed them all and came to another land seeking a way to live—a great sinner!...

“My gentle Tamil woman... a great feast to my heart... I am the cruel wretch who dared even to sell the anklet she wore upon her lotus feet—that is how I explain myself!......

“This is a heart steeped in Tamil! Formed in the womb of Mother Tamil, I crawled upon the soil of the sacred land... I was not created with a darkened mind to practise theft—this I declare!... I am certain that I can obtain justice in the court of the great king Nedunchezhiyan, whom every land praises! Take me to the king at once!....”

“The king, indeed! Do you think he is your house watchman, to meet whenever it occurs to you?....”

“He is the guardian of the country!... Guardian of justice, integrity, virtue, love and Tamil—Nedunchezhiyan!... Not even half an atom of injustice will he permit in his rule... so I have heard the whole world praise him. Let that great Pandyan king look at my face!... Will he see the marks of a thief?... Will he point out the shadow of a stain?... Before Nedunchezhiyan, the sovereign who, by the signs on a face—

—pronounces justice, I shall stand!... I accept his verdict!... Take me to him!....”

“You think you can deceive the king... you crafty-handed thief!... Our Pandyan king will not even raise his eyes to look at the faces of sinners like you!”.......

“Why!... One who looks at you will not look at me?....”

“You arrogant fool!.... Confess your crime..... Otherwise your life will not escape!”...

“I was not born in a lineage that lives after honour is lost... I was born in a warrior Tamil clan!....”

“I too am of Tamil stock!” says the officer arrogantly.

“You... an axe-handle come to destroy your own clan! A man without principles!... A jackal! An exception to a warrior lineage!....”

“Your tongue will be cut off; you will be dragged through the middle of the street like a dog!”...

“Your country’s fame will spread in all four directions!... Do that noble deed!....”

The chief goldsmith intervenes. “Why such insolence from that mouth of yours!... As though stealing were not enough, arrogant bluster too?... You bloated brute!.......... Tell the truth without talking back!...”

“Goldsmith!” Kovalan cries, overcome with emotion. The goldsmith says, “You are indeed a thief!...” The hundred goldsmiths join together and shout at Kovalan, “A thief indeed! A thief indeed!!”

Overcome by a flood of emotion, Kovalan cries, “Thief! I am a thief! Kannagi!... Your husband is a thief! My ten-and-a-half-touch gold!... That is the verdict of Pandya land! Am I a thief, my dear?... With tears brimming, you bade me farewell, Kannagi; did you know that such a stain would come upon me, darling!

“Sweet honey!... A fitting punishment! A fitting reward for the wretch who cast you into the street dust as *veenai*!... Thief! Thief!!... Thief!!!... Ayyo! Your heart has already withered from longing... how will it bear the news that this vile word has fallen upon me!... You married me! You thought you would know happiness!... I turned your life into a volcano!... As though that were not enough, I have made you a target for the townspeople to sneer, ‘Kannagi, wife of a thief!’ O morning star! Peerless gem! You should sit amid mansion halls and towers, plucking the *makara yaazh*... and while the gentle breeze lulls, I should sleep in your lap... But here I am in the streets of great Madurai... struck by the *kanai* called ‘thief’, I cry out! I lament!...

“They say I am a thief, Kannagi—a thief!...

You tell me...

Am I a thief...?

Am I a thief?...

Here is the thief!!

He is the thief!!!....

He is the thief!!!”

He approaches the officer and points him out. The officer raises his sword. “What did you say?” he demands, and swings it to sever Kovalan’s head. The cut lands on his neck.

“Ah... Kannagi!” Kovalan cries, writhes and falls.
