That was the conversation that took place when compositor Kandasami showed Vedanthi Press manager Velayutham Pillai a specimen sheet of a ‘notice’ he had typeset!

Kandasami tore up the proof sheet that the manager had found fault with, took the original and read it once more.

Pearl-like letters. A look of pity showed on Kandasami’s face.

**“Thirelapathi Amman Temple Maha Kumbabhishekam”**—this was the heading of the advertisement. Under the programme was the line: **“Sri Jagath Yogananda Swamigal will deliver a discourse on Panchali Suyamvaram.”** Kandasami bit his lips. A little mocking laugh was pulled out of him, followed by an ugly deep sigh.

“I have been in this press for five years now. Rather than setting type for this obscene heap of puranic rubbish, it would be far better to hang myself! Ah, Tamil letters! Flowers are strung into garlands for the goat-kid sacrificed to Mahakali; they are also woven into wedding garlands. In the same way, you too weave honey-sweet poems and prose that shows the way to life into the form of books. And puranas overflowing with falsehood, trickery and bundles of lies also become your creations. Yes—the gathering of letters is just like a gathering of flowers that is handled and worn away in human hands!”

The waves in his heart subsided. Kandasami’s eyes opened wide, as though he had discovered something. Once again he looked at the ‘original’ in his hand. Every letter in it stared at him; they pierced into his heart. He muttered under his breath: “Maha kumbabhishekam..........hm......foolish world......” Contempt

and disgust scored lines across his face. Again he spoke to himself.

“A thieving Chettiar is going to conduct a kumbabhishekam!......He gathers his gang and reduces the town to ruin. And these blind people believe him. I know...I know the story of how he raped Ekambaram’s wife! Wasn’t it by laying hands on Periyanna Padaiyachi’s cash-box while the man was at death’s door that his house acquired the title ‘Periya Pannai’? Wasn’t it by prying out and selling the diamonds of Vedalapuram Venkatachalapathi’s keerthi that he built that enormous house! Is it enough to hide all this by conducting a kumbabhishekam?......You hardened sinner! If there is such a thing as hell, a cobra ought to be set upon every nerve in your body!....And now you have come to build a temple—a temple! Ramanathan Chettiar, wait! Wait!—I will settle this with you. I do not care even if my life goes.”

Kandasami’s head rose proudly. He stood like an ancient Dravidian warrior who, facing the commander of the opposing army, declares with pride, “Take up your sword.” In his mind Ramanathan Chettiar stood before him!...Then he rapidly began setting type. His mouth kept muttering.

“Is it done, compositor?” the manager barked.

Kandasami took the proof sheet he had typeset and held it out.

A fine frame, a beautiful arrangement. The very design of the advertisement sheet was distinctive.

A car stopped at the entrance of the press. Ramanathan Chettiar got out. “Done?” he asked with an eager look. The white silk cloth that had slipped down instead of staying on his silk shirt was picked up and placed back on Chettiar’s shoulder

by the servant who came behind him! Seeing the condition of that slave-like servant, Kandasami’s throat tightened. The manager stood with his mouth open, waiting to hear what criticism might come from Chettiar.

“Besh! It looks grand; print ten thousand notices.”

Chettiar looked at his servant. “Hey! Rathinam, stay here and take all the notices...and...”

“I’ll paste them on the walls, master.”

“You fool!......This isn’t a ‘wall poster’. Take them street by street and distribute them; all ten thousand notices must be gone by this evening...yes.”

“Your order, master.”

The manager bowed his head and sent him off; Ramanathan Chettiar’s car flew away.

“Hey! Machineman! Print ten thousand ‘notices’ at once. Use ‘blue’ ink...hm...Hey! Compositor!...Get the notice ready...quickly.”

The manager, jumping about in his haste, left for his meal. Kandasami went inside with the proof sheet. He took the composed type and fixed it into the iron frame. A slight tremor could be seen in his hands. He pulled out a few of the letters he had first set. He picked other letters......and fitted them into the empty spaces.

The iron frame went into the printing machine. One....two......three...naaru........a thousand.......ten thousand..........The ‘notices’ were finished. Kandasami himself gathered and bundled them. The servant Rathinam set off carrying the load of advertisements. Under Chettiar’s order—

The next day was the kumbabhishekam. An unbearable crowd. Chettiar, decked in full ceremonial splendour, arrived by car with his companions. A group of women arrived in another car. He looked once at the crowd. “**All this is my** greatness,” said his raised head. A dismissive smile—one he deliberately summoned! His eyes, spraying arrogance, widened.

The crowd had seen him. A great roar!......“Hey, Kamanathan Chettiar......Kamanathan Chettiar”....Such a tremendous noise arose. Unnecessary applause. A single wave of mocking laughter. In between came questions: “Kamanathan Chettiar! Where is Bogananda Swamigal?” “What fitting names, man......Kamanathan—Boganandar...” Such commentary. Again applause and laughter. “For Kamanathan Chettiar, who rapes whatever women he sees”—came a lone voice. It was followed by cries of “Jai! Jai!” By then, children who understood nothing took the blinking, bewildered Chettiar for a rich madman and threw little stones at him. All of it together grew into a great disturbance—until the police resorted to a baton charge. Chettiar’s face seemed to drip lamp oil. “I don’t want the kumbabhishekam or anything else; turn the car around!” he roared. The cars turned back under a rain of stones. Thinking Chettiar was fleeing in defeat, the crowd broke into even louder applause!......

According to the priests’ decision that the ‘lagnam’ must not be missed, the kumbabhishekam took place even without Chettiar. As listed in the programme, Yogananda Swamigal arrived at Chettiar’s house for the discourse. Chettiar lay there as the very image of sorrow. Yogananda Swamigal’s face too was exploding with fury.

“Chettiar-vaal...how many days have you been planning to humiliate me?” the Swamigal poured out in anger.

“What, Swami!...You are speaking without making yourself understood,” Chettiar replied calmly.

“Look at this.”

Yogananda Swamigal took out the advertisement sheet and flung it at Chettiar.

“Thirelapathi Amman Temple Maha Kumbabhishekam.” Chettiar read it.

“What is wrong with this, Swami?”

“Read all of it, sir.”

Chettiar began to read.

“‘Sri Jagath Bogananda Swamigal will immediately beg on the subject of Panchali vibasaaram’......Ayyo!....Yoganandar has become Boganandar! Panchali Suyamvaram has become Panchali vibachaaram! ‘Will deliver a discourse’ has become ‘will immediately beg’!......Which scoundrel did this?” Chettiar growled like a lion. Yogananda Swamigal’s hands and legs shook even though his mouth could not speak. At that moment the accountant, looking as though he had discovered something new, cried, “Look here, look here,” and showed them the ‘notice’.

“Thus, servant of Ambal, Kamanathan Chettiar”—those were the block letters! Ramanathan Chettiar sprang up and began a terrible dance of rage. He ground his teeth rapidly.

“The printing-office fellow has printed only the truth,” the accountant thought to himself—he did not say it.

“Hey, driver, bring the car!” Chettiar ordered.

The car stopped in front of Vedanthi Press. The manager’s room became like a brassware shop into which elephants had charged.

“Hey, compositor! You!” manager Velayutham Pillai called in a fury.

Compositor Kandasami came and stood before him.

A mighty slap—Kandasami was struck by the manager. Kandasami, who had been hit, neither cried nor grew angry; he laughed.

“What are you laughing at, you thick-headed fellow?” The manager threw the notice at his face.

“Nothing, sir. I have set it exactly as in the original.”

“Original? What original, man! Does the original say ‘Panchali vibachaaram’?”

“Yes....The true original for this is the Bharatham. Not this handwritten notice!....In the Bharatham original, Panchali is portrayed as the wife of five men and as a woman who desired Karna as a sixth husband. Going by that description, ‘Panchali vibachaaram’ seemed to me a truer original than ‘Panchali Suyamvaram’.

“And that is not all....This Chettiar is a beast of lust—a kaandaa mirugam! That is the original of his life history! So I composed his name as Kamanathan Chettiar.

“The Swami coming to deliver the discourse is a boga-poona, a cat of pleasure. By day he is the supreme devotee. By night he is the supreme sinner. I did not wish to alter this true original either, so I printed Bogananda Swamigal. Is that a crime?”—Kandasami laughed loudly.

“Ah!”—the manager’s mouth fell open.

“Wait. Weren’t you the one who told me to put it exactly as in the original?” Kandasami laughed again and walked out of Vedanthi Press.

Chettiar and the manager looked at each other.
