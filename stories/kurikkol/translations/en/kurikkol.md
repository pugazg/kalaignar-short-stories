# The Goal

> **English translation of the verified Tamil archival reading.** Controlling source: 1987 collection `கலைஞர் சொன்ன குட்டிக் கதைகள்`, scan **31 / printed page 30 → upper scan 32 / printed page 31**.

An example from the story-filled Bharata, whose ideas are not to my liking.

Arjuna was a famous archer. He received the special name Kandeeban when he was very young. He won that fame while he was a student under Drona. When the Pandavas and Kauravas were students under Drona and their training had ended, Drona called both groups in order to examine and test them. He showed them a large tree and the bird sitting in that tree, and asked each of them to draw his bow.

Looking at Duryan standing with his bow drawn, Drona asked, “What do you see?” As he went on describing, “I see the tree—I see the leaves—,” Drona told him he was not wanted. He asked all the warriors, and each said, “I see the tree—I see the leaves—I see the branch.” Setting all of them aside, he finally called Arjuna and asked, “You look! What do you see?” Arjuna said, “I can see only the tip of the arrow fixed to my bow

<!-- anthology scan: 32; printed page: 31; source span: upper -->

and the neck of the bird sitting on the branch.” As soon as he said that, he shot the arrow. The bird's neck fell to the ground.

Therefore, if there is a goal, one's entire attention must remain on that goal and must not wander to either side. If Arjuna stands as the example of one who succeeded in such a test, that was in his very young days! Once he became a little older, he began looking to this side and that—you know all the directions in which he looked. I do not wish to go into all those stories of Alli and Pavalakodi. I have told this only to point to the marvels Arjuna performed in his youth.

## Translation note

Source names such as `காண்டீபன்`, `துரியன்`, `அல்லி`, and `பவளக்கொடி` are conservatively transliterated as Kandeeban, Duryan, Alli and Pavalakodi rather than silently standardized from outside versions of the epic.
