# Gajini Muhammad and Poet Pardosi

> **English translation of the verified Tamil archival reading.** Controlling source: 1987 collection `கலைஞர் சொன்ன குட்டிக் கதைகள்`, lower scan **18 → 19 → upper 20**. Source spellings such as `கஜினிமுகமது` and `பார்டோசி` are represented conservatively rather than replaced from outside editions.

<!-- anthology scan: 18; printed page: 17; lower shared span -->

The great poet called Pardosi was a friend of Gajini Muhammad. Gajini Muhammad said to his friend, “I have seen so many battlefields; I have achieved so many victories; I have amassed so many possessions; I own mansions, halls and towers. But what I consider higher than all these is a poet praising me. Therefore, Poet Pardosi, you must weave such a garland of praise in verse and give it to me.”

The poet hesitated a little. Gajini Muhammad immediately said, “If you write such a garland of verse praising me, I will ‘give’ you sixty thousand gold coins as a reward.” Excited that sixty thousand gold coins would be his, Pardosi immediately agreed, began writing the poems, and came to the ceremonial hall to sing the jeweled garland of poems he had composed before Gajini Muhammad. Gajini Muhammad was overcome with delight.

<!-- anthology scan: 19; printed page: 18 -->

The poet stood there expecting the sixty thousand gold coins that were to be given to him. “Why are you standing there?” Gajini Muhammad asked.

Poet Pardosi replied, “You said you would give me sixty thousand gold coins! I am waiting to receive them before I leave.”

Gajini Muhammad immediately said, “Sixty thousand gold coins? Who said such a thing? If you want, I will give you sixty thousand silver coins; take them and go.”

On hearing this, the poet said, “Who wants your silver coins?” and went home. At home he withered in grief; he realized that he had been deceived. As a result of that deception, his chest tightened; his heart burst. He died.

After he died, while his body was being carried out of his house, a cart loaded with sixty thousand gold coins arrived from Gajini Muhammad's palace. The men who came went back and told Gajini Muhammad that the poet had died.

Gajini cried, “When he finished singing his garland of praise for me, I did it only as a joke, to subject him to a sudden deception. I said it only after I had decided to give him sixty thousand gold coins. But before they could reach him, I have lost Poet Pardosi!”

It is the same with Bharathiyar. Today we are celebrating his centenary. After Bharathiyar died, we built a memorial hall; even after that, we—

<!-- anthology scan: 20; printed page: 19; upper shared span -->

—praised him; extolled him. What can all this be compared to? It is like Gajini Muhammad who, after deceiving the poet for sport by saying he would give the reward, and after the deceived poet had died, sent word: “Here are sixty thousand gold coins.” In the same way, the country today is celebrating Bharathiyar's centenary.
