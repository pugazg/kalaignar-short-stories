# Ice Katti

A king, it is said, called his ministers and asked them. My friends! We draw up so many schemes and toil for the people. Yet they say these schemes are not reaching the people. What is the reason?” he is said to have asked. One minister gave an answer—not with words, but through an action:

He asked for a block of ice to be brought. He asked that it be placed in the king’s hand. The king took the block of ice and held it, then asked, “What am I to do?” “Pass it, in order, through the hands of each minister,” he said. Accordingly, it was passed from one minister’s hand to another in sequence. Then came the senior officials, next the middle-level officials,

and after them the junior officials. In this way, the block of ice passed along until it reached the lowest-ranking employee at the very end. The block of ice that had been placed in the king’s hand changed hands through so many people that, by the time it finally reached the hand of the person who was meant to benefit from it, only a single drop of water remained. That is how the beneficiary of a scheme stands at the very end. What ought to reach him does not reach him. (Loud applause) “Your Majesty! This is how all our schemes reach the people,” said the minister. No one should turn our schemes into blocks of ice like that. They must reach the people directly from the king’s hand. I firmly assure you that, as soon as the government announces something, I will stay awake and keep watch.
