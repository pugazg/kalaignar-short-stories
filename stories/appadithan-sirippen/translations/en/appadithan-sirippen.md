# Appadithan Sirippen

<!-- source scan 9; printed page 7 -->

A king and queen sat smiling gently on the balcony of their palace. They called the servants and asked them to bring fruit. From among the fruit they took a fine orange. The queen peeled it and gave it to the king. He swallowed one segment, then took another and fed it to the queen. The peel of the fruit was then thrown outside. A beggar passing along the street picked up the orange peel and bit into it. Seeing this, the queen grew angry. “He is eating the peel we threw away after eating the fruit? Does he not even know that this belongs to the palace?” she fumed. At once the king sent his guard to whip the beggar. The guard ran downstairs and, saying, “How dare you eat the peel of the fruit the king ate?” struck him with the whip. The beggar took the blows and laughed aloud. The king was astonished. He himself came down and questioned him. The beggar answered, “Yes!

“If I get this many lashes merely for eating the peel of that fruit, I am laughing because I am thinking how many lashes must fall on you for eating the whole fruit,” the beggar is said to have told the king and queen as he looked at them.
