# The Story of Poet Pugazhendhi

> **English translation of the verified Tamil archival reading.** Controlling source: 1987 collection `கலைஞர் சொன்ன குட்டிக் கதைகள்`, lower scan **27 / printed page 26 → scan 28 / printed page 27**.

<!-- anthology scan: 27; printed page: 26; source span: lower -->

Our friend Thamalingam's younger daughter, Selvi Thenmozhi, and Mr Duraisamy's second son, Pugazhendhi, are today stepping into married life as life-partners in the presence of all of us.

When I recalled the word “honey” and the name Pugazhendhi, the story of the poet Pugazhendhi also came to mind. Poet Pugazhendhi sang a beautiful verse before the king.

<!-- anthology scan: 28; printed page: 27 -->

He sang a verse containing the line, “The bee blows the white conch that is the jasmine.” The king praised the song, and everyone in the court spoke in admiration. As everyone delighted in the beautiful phrase, “the bee blows the white conch of the jasmine,” a poet there named Ottakoothar said, “I reject that verse. It is faulty. It contains an error of meaning.”

When the king asked how, Ottakoothar said, “Pugazhendhi says the bee blows the jasmine flower as though it were a white conch. But I ask: if one is to blow a conch, one must blow from its rear. Does not the bee sit on the front of the jasmine flower? How can this fit?” Pugazhendhi immediately replied, “This bee, intoxicated after drinking honey, does not know which is the front and which is the back when it blows.”

So here too the bridegroom Pugazhendhi is seated in the intoxication of drinking “honey.” There is therefore nothing surprising in my blessing them and taking part in the celebration at Thamalingam's home.

With the feeling that they will not be angry if I do not speak for long at a family occasion of ours, I bless the bride and groom to live a full and worthy life.

## Translation note

The two source variants of the jasmine/conch phrase are translated separately rather than silently harmonized. The `தேன்` / Thenmozhi wordplay is retained through “honey” where the speech invokes it.
