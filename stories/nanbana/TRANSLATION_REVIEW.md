## Difficult / source-sensitive choices

The full `POSSIBLE_ERRORS_FOR_REVIEW.md` queue was read before translation. Queue entries were not silently corrected.

- source-separated `சூழ் நிலைகள்` is translated by its immediate sense as **“Circumstances conspired against us”**; the Tamil layer remains unchanged.
- `குடும்பம் ஆட்டங்கொடுக்காமல்` is represented conservatively as the family moving along **without being shaken on the economic front**.
- source spelling `முந்நூறு` is translated by its clear numerical value as **three hundred rupees**; no Tamil spelling normalization was made.
- `‘இருமலர் ஓர் அரும்பு’` is rendered **“Two Blossoms, One Bud”**, preserving the playful family-photograph caption and the following joke about the number of children.
- `ஒரு கட்டையைக் கட்டிக்கொண்டதும்` is deliberately kept as the strange comic metaphor **“now that he has tied a log to himself”** rather than being silently flattened to “got married.”
- `புறநானூறு` is transliterated **Purananuru** without importing outside literary explanation.
- the verified but semantically unusual `மாணலோகச் சீட்டு!` remains unresolved in the human recheck queue. English therefore uses the conservative mixed rendering **“A ticket to Maanalogam!”**: `சீட்டு` is translated, while the uncertain `மாணலோகம்` element is transliterated rather than guessed or normalized.
- `மனந்திருந்திக்` remains unchanged in Tamil; in English its immediate narrative function is represented minimally as **“have changed my mind.”**
- the two-line Kural quotation is translated directly from the repository Tamil as **“To punish those who have done you harm, / do them good, so that they are shamed.”** No published outside translation was imported.
- `தங்கை முறை ஆகிவிடுவாளா?` is rendered **“Does Saradha, who divorced me, immediately become my younger sister?”**, preserving the final kinship-based reversal rather than adding explanatory narration.

## Structural / rhetorical review

- the story’s epistolary structure is preserved: Babu’s long playful complaint, separator, then Sekar’s very short reply;
- Babu’s teasing about marriage, children, economic security, the anti-Hindi agitation and his old anti-marriage rhetoric remains conversational rather than formalized;
- the five-line anti-marriage passage remains a separate display block;
- the Kural remains a two-line display quotation;
- the punchline is not explained in advance: only Sekar’s final reply reveals that Babu’s bride Saradha is Sekar’s former wife;
- no outside biographical, political or literary context was inserted into the translation body.
