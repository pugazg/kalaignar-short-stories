An elder brother and a younger brother were driving a donkey to their place of work. They were washermen.

<!-- source scan 20; printed page 18 -->

As they went along, two mischief-makers deliberately followed behind them. “Why are both of you walking? The donkey is going along with nothing on it. Couldn’t the elder one sit on it while the younger one drives the donkey?” they asked. At once, they seated the elder brother on the donkey and the younger brother drove it along. After they had gone some distance, the mischief-makers looked at them again and asked, “You are the elder one—have you no compassion? Can the elder brother ride the donkey while making the younger one walk?” At once they said, “Is that so?” The elder brother got down, they seated the younger one, and continued.

After they had gone a little farther, the mischief-makers again looked at them and asked, “There is so much room on the donkey’s back! Couldn’t both of you get on and ride?” “All right,” they said, and both climbed onto the donkey. The donkey was accustomed to carrying clothes. Could it carry them? Unable to bear the load, it lay down.

At once the mischief-makers are said to have told them, “Until now the donkey has carried you. From now on, you carry the donkey.”

Immediately the two men tied a pole to the donkey’s legs, lifted it, and put it on their shoulders

and carried it along. On the way they came to a bridge. Plenty of water was flowing in the river. They had tied the donkey upside down and were carrying it across the bridge. Would the donkey keep still? It gave a sudden leap and fell into the river. In the end, all they gained was the loss of the donkey. In the same way, if one listens to everything every passer-by says, what happens is the story of losing the donkey.
