There is a verse in *Nal Vazhi*:

“A rock that does not yield to a long iron bar
yields to the root of a green tree.”

What does this mean? We use a crowbar to break a rock. Even when we use a crowbar to break rock, after two or three blows its sharp edge becomes blunt. We sharpen it and then break the rock with it. Such a crowbar, made of good long, heavy iron,

can itself be blunted by the rock. That is how hard the rock is to break.

But the root of a small plant in that very rock enters every crevice and gap, works its way into every opening, sprouts and takes root within the rock, grows into a tree and splits the rock apart. So should one use a crowbar to split the rock? Or should one break the rock with the maturity and subtlety of that green tree's root? Having learned that art from Anna, I consider it my duty to tell it to my younger brothers as well.
