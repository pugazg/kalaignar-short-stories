## Difficult / source-sensitive choices

The persistent human-review queue was read before translation. The English follows the verified Tamil and does not silently import readings from another edition.

- the opening Bharathidasan verse preserves the source’s three-line archival structure. `சதிமிதிக்கும்`, `வதங்கவிலாச்சண்பகத்து` and `சாடை` were translated through their immediate poetic images without changing the Tamil source.
- the opaque source phrase `நாறுவது நடன நிகழ்ச்சி` was not silently regularized to an assumed ordinal. The English conservatively retains **naaruvathu dance performance** rather than inventing a number unsupported by the verified source.
- `அத்தனைநாள் கடந்தவம்` is represented by the source’s humorous image of **prolonged penance** for Amudha’s glance.
- `முன்னேடி` is translated as **forerunner**, preserving Arulnambi’s habitual prominence at social/cultural events.
- `கிறு கிறுக்க` is represented as **giddy**, and `இன்பபுரிக்கு` as **the city of pleasure**, keeping the source’s erotic-comic register without adding detail.
- `காலக்கடன்களை` is rendered conservatively as **morning obligations**, not replaced with a more specific routine not stated in the source.
- colloquial `அமுதா இருக்குதா?`, `சேச்சே!`, and other telephone speech are kept colloquial in English.
- `பகல் விருந்து ரசிகுமா?` is rendered as **Would a daytime feast have any relish?**, retaining the suggestive double sense without expanding it.
- `தணலான` is represented as the body **burning like embers**.
- `அடுக்குளப்பக்கம்` is translated by immediate spatial sense as **toward the kitchen**; the Tamil form remains untouched.
- `வண்ணமொழிகேட்டு` is rendered as hearing **richly coloured words**, retaining the figurative phrasing.
- source-corrected `இவனத் தெரியுமா?` is represented as **Do you know this boy?**; the earlier provisional reading is not reintroduced.
- `நம்பப் பயலா?` is rendered minimally as **Our little fellow?**, preserving Arulnambi’s familiar tone without forcing a stronger relationship than the source supports.

## Structural and rhetorical review

- Arulnambi’s public cultural stature and his refusal to accept being considered old are established before his fixation on Amudha;
- small physical contacts at the garland, milk bowl, dinner table and albums are narrated as his escalating interpretation of her attention, not as objective confirmation of romance;
- the telephone and dinner dialogue preserves the source’s food/flesh innuendo without adding sexual detail;
- the absence of the family during dinner sustains Arulnambi’s expectation until their return;
- Amudha’s introduction of her son and instruction to greet Arulnambi as `தாத்தா` provide the decisive comic reversal;
- the empty hall shows that Arulnambi has left before Amudha returns with the child, and her victorious smile remains the final narrative beat;
- no outside biographical identification or interpretation of the characters was added.
