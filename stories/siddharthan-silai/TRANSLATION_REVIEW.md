## Structural and rhetorical review

- the opening idealized portrait establishes the woman’s beauty and the husband’s apparently absolute devotion before his unexplained departure;
- the friend directs her to a Buddha shrine as a conventional source of relief;
- the apparent statue speech undercuts that expectation by pointing to Siddhartha’s own renunciation of wife and child while preaching love;
- the husband’s reveal makes the statue’s speech a deliberate human impersonation rather than a supernatural event;
- his final statement separates Siddhartha’s world-serving principle from later expectations that a deified figure should answer private petitions and accept offerings;
- no outside Buddhist doctrine, biography or apologetic/counter-apologetic explanation was inserted.
