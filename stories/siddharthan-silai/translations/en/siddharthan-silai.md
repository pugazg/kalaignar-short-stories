# Siddharthan Silai

<!-- anthology scan: 250; printed page: 241 -->

Eyes like the kendai fish. Hair like a black beetle. All the redness of a sky that spills a little vermilion smile seemed gathered on her lips...on her cheeks! Her slender waist and the vast beauty of youth competed with each other in a golden doll so delicate that one wondered whether she could bear her own form or bend beneath it. Like foam arriving wave after wave, every word she shed was a drop of honey fragrant with taste. Her fear that even the earth might be hurt became clear in the way she walked. When she laughed, the moonlight that scattered from her smile seemed to make false the truth that pearls are born in shells! Those who said sweet music could arise only from the yazh and the flute would hide away, listen to her song, and change their opinion: “Who said so? There is song in the yazh and flute too.”

The world itself congratulated the man who had fathered her: what a rare fortune he had received! “I bow to those who gave birth to you. Tamil! My life! My painting! My body shall not remain even a moment apart from you!” Taking such a vow, he stayed beside her. He took pride in saying that all the beauty of literature and all the taste of epic poetry could be found in living with her.

How could such a man ever leave her? The whole town sank into astonishment. Her eyes filled with water; a situation arose in which she was left alone.

“Has he tired of that honeyed face? Has he forgotten that heavenly countenance? My parrot, why has this sorrow come to you?” the women of the town asked.

“I gave him my heart. I lay believing that he was my very breath of life. I never imagined that a desert’s scorching wind would blow upon a flower that had known the comfort of dew. For two weeks his mind was not steady. I understood it from his speech. I did not know then that it would come to this, friend!” cried the Tamil maiden.

“Do not grieve! Do not shed tears! I will tell you a way—listen! South of the river, in a beautiful grove, there is a temple of the Buddha. Go there and pour out your grievance; your husband will return. Do not stand here—go!” said her dearest friend.

So she went.

“Form of grace! Flame of love! Great Siddhartha who did what was hard to do! Under the shade of the Bodhi tree you gave coolness to the world. Today, by your shade, drive away my grief. My handsome lord, my master, my life-companion, my beloved who said he would never give me the pain of separation—he is not with me now! Where is he? Why did he go? This poor woman does not know! Pure one, wipe away my sorrow! Great one who showed the people of the world a way to live! Ocean of compassion! With the hand of your wave, wet this parched shore. Make my heart happy!”

She wept. She worshipped. She lit a lamp and stood before the statue shedding tears. The statue spoke. She closed her eyes and listened:

“Woman! You have come to tell me of the grief caused by your husband leaving you. What sense have you? A beautiful lightning-like woman—and the dear

son sleeping beside her—I left both of them longing and took up renunciation in order to preach ‘love’. The lesson I gave the world...love!...love!...love!... Yet I myself came away without showing that very love to the companion who lived trusting me, or to the child she bore. I have no power to understand your grief at being separated from your husband. Go—go!”

She heard the voice and cried, “Ayyo, Bhagavan!”

She heard laughter. She opened her eyes. Her husband stepped out from behind the statue.

“Beloved!” she cried and sprang toward him.

Embracing her, he said, “My life! I only played a little game with you! And already you have become the very form of suffering! Come, come, let us go.”

“Siddhartha’s grace!” she said.

“What he bestowed was a lofty principle that can help the world live. He is not there to bring you and me together, fulfil the prayers of the world and collect offerings as wages. Alas! They have made him into a god—evil! Evil!!” he said.

“Then who was speaking?” she asked.

“Not the statue. I was!” he said.

He embraced her.
