# Kumanan Who Went to the Forest

> **English translation of the verified Tamil archival reading.** Controlling source: 1987 collection `கலைஞர் சொன்ன குட்டிக் கதைகள்`, lower scan **44 / printed page 43 → upper scan 45 / printed page 44**.

<!-- anthology scan: 44; printed page: 43; source span: lower -->

As employees of the consumer-goods trading corporation, you have conducted the state conference of your renowned union with grandeur and distinction and passed a resolution containing 29 demands.

Friends who spoke here pointed out that if your demands are to be fulfilled, a thirtieth demand too must be made—that is, a resolution that the present government should change and Karunanidhi should again become Chief Minister must also be passed.

Though I am not the Chief Minister who can fulfil these demands, I am duty-bound to tell you firmly that I will be the first to voice these demands.

I am proud to think that you have courageously placed 29 demands and have also acquired strong shoulders capable of fighting for them.

When, with regret, I say that I cannot—could not—fulfil them, the story of Kumanan comes to my mind.

Kumanan, in accordance with the wish of his younger brother, renounced the crown and lived in the forest. Still not satisfied, his younger brother thought that Kumanan's being alive would be a nuisance to him and announced a price on his head.

In this situation, a poet who knew nothing of all this came to the forest and sang of Kumanan. Then:

<!-- anthology scan: 45; printed page: 44; source span: upper -->

“O poet of rare verse, who did not come that day,
why have you come this day, suffering, and reached me?
Take this head and give it into my younger brother's hand;
receive its price and rid yourself of the disease of poverty!”

Just as Kumanan said that, I am ready to say the same.

Though this may not be that day, you know that this day and that day are days of equal worth to me.

Therefore, for your sake I will be ready for any sacrifice, and I will be ready to argue in whatever way is needed—to fight in whatever way is needed—to have your demands fulfilled. I give you the assurance that the DMK, to which I belong, will stand by you.

## Translation note

The four-line Kumanan verse is translated from the verified 1987 reading without normalizing source forms such as `வந்தில`, `வந்துநீ`, `தம்பிகைக்`, `பெற்றுஉன்`, or `வறுமைநோய்`.
