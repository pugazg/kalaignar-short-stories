# The Leader's Gift

In the battlefield where the army becomes foliage, red blood gathers like water and elephants tread a terrible mire, the war drum has thundered for the enemies’ crowns to be planted in the earth. The summons has come to that warrior.

“See the army’s roar, my dear—give me leave without hindrance, my woman!” he pleads. He is a warrior who considers every day without an honourable wound a wasted day. The slender beauty, graceful of eye, purses lips that scatter a pearl-like smile, gives him a kiss and, embracing him, says, “Return victorious, my lord,” wiping her tears on his shoulder without letting him see them.

“Bring me the spear,” he says—she gives him her gaze. “Where is the blood-stained sword?” he asks—she gives him her red lips. “There is no time, woman, not this moment! My inseparable companion! The leader will be standing ready, looking for me! Give me the spear, give me leave, peerless beauty!” Saying so, with the sluice gate

opened, letting a flood of love pour forth, the warrior began to walk toward the ranks of the great fighters.

On the way his mother saw him and blessed him. “Come back victorious, my tiger,” she said. “I know the news that the leader bears a little anger toward you. If you hide it, will your mother not know, little brother? The line of soldiers waiting to give their heads for the greatness of the motherland is ready. Go and do your duty, my dear,” she said, kissing the wound he had received earlier on his chest.

The warrior went to battle, crushed the enemy and consigned them to bone-dust. In the bloody mire, warriors of both sides were made ready for their final journey. When a message flew in that final victory would belong to the enemy, he asked, “Are we blind though we have eyes?” and our foot flew across the field like a whirlwind. Alone, before the eyes of the leader, he destroyed the strength of the enemy army, swept away the smoke-cloud of defeat and stood firm. He sowed the seed of victory. Alas! In the end he became the prey of an arrow that came from hiding!

The commander who won the victory saw the fallen warrior and broke into a cry...

The guardian too, who had held a difference of opinion in his heart because of a small bitterness caused by the mischief of lesser men, now bathed with tears the bull-like warrior who lay fallen on the field.

The dying warrior says:

“Leader! Warriors should beg, if they must, to obtain such deaths on the battlefield—deaths that make the eyes of the leader who guarded them overflow with tears.”
