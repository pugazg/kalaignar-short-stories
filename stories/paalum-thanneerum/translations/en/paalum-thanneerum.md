# Milk and Water

> **English translation of the verified Tamil archival reading.** Controlling source: 1987 collection `கலைஞர் சொன்ன குட்டிக் கதைகள்`, lower scan **32 / printed page 31 → scan 33 / printed page 32 → upper scan 34 / printed page 33**.

<!-- anthology scan: 32; printed page: 31; source span: lower -->

Anbil, taking something I said jokingly long ago—when I said, “What, man, are you an all-party leader?”—said that it was not enough for all the party leaders gathered here to support him; M.G.R.'s party too would support him, and the Indira Congress party too would support him.

<!-- anthology scan: 33; printed page: 32 -->

When he said that everyone would support me, it did not mean, “I need do no more work.” Nor did it mean that he could go to Lalgudi or Anbil and take rest.

If he believes—and we too believe—what Anbil said, that M.G.R.'s party, the Indira Congress party and all the parties will vote for him, an old story comes to mind.

A king once ordered everyone in the town to bring one pot of milk each and pour it into a tank built in the fort.

“If I take a pot of water and pour it in instead of a pot of milk, who is going to know! So many people will bring milk and pour it in. If we pour one pot of water among all that, who can find out?” Every householder thought the same way, and every one of them brought water and poured it in. When the tank was finally opened, there was only water in it and no milk at all. In the same way, if Anbil, instead of relying on all the party leaders seated here, trusts that M.G.R.'s party and the Indira Congress party will vote for him, all of them too

<!-- anthology scan: 34; printed page: 33; source span: upper -->

will leave him and go away. I am duty-bound to warn Anbil. The warning is not for Anbil! I am duty-bound to warn and appeal to all the fine activists working for the DMK in the Thanjavur constituency.
