# The Child and the Parrot

> **English translation of the verified Tamil archival reading.** Controlling source: 1987 collection `கலைஞர் சொன்ன குட்டிக் கதைகள்`, lower scan **48 / printed page 47 → scan 49 / printed page 48**.

<!-- anthology scan: 48; printed page: 47; source span: lower -->

We were once the very people whom the people of Tamil Nadu placed on Tamil Nadu's throne. We were the people who held ministerial responsibility for nine years; I was the one who served as Chief Minister for seven years. Because I do not hold that office today, I am not prepared to forget you. In the meantime, you may have developed some suspicion toward us; or some anger. You may have formed the mistaken notion that we committed a wrong.

We have read in many lessons of a Brahmin woman who reared a parrot. Leaving that parrot as a guard for her child, she would go out on various errands.

<!-- anthology scan: 49; printed page: 48 -->

One day, after putting the child to sleep in a cradle, that Brahmin woman left the parrot on guard, took a pot and went to the pond to draw water. Before she returned, a snake came down from above the cradle and reached the point of striking the child. The parrot saw it, immediately pounced, killed the snake and saved the child, and then ran outside to tell the victorious news that it had saved the child.

Because it had killed the snake, the parrot's mouth was covered in blood. The woman who had gone to the pond returned home. The parrot ran out from the doorway, and she saw its mouth. She became suspicious. “Ah! The parrot I left here for protection has killed our tender child, hasn't it? That is why there is blood on its mouth,” she thought. She acted in haste. She brought the water-filled pot in her hand down on the parrot's head. She killed the parrot. When she went inside and looked, the child was lying in the cradle, smiling. The snake bitten by the parrot lay there in pieces.

In just the same way, as that Brahmin woman misunderstood the parrot and killed it, you defeated the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam in the last election.

“Come a little further inside and you will know! You will understand whether the reason there was blood in the parrot's mouth was really that it had bitten the infant.” Therefore we invite you to come inside. We invite you to understand the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam; we invite you to understand the principles of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam.

## Translation note

Library stamp and handwritten accession marks on scan 49 are non-story source marks and are not translated into the narrative. Verified source forms such as `எங்கள்மீதேதோ`, `ஏதோஒரு`, `முகந்துவர`, `அந்தக்குழந்தையைத்`, `வெற்றிச்செய்தியைச்`, and `பச்சிளங்` remain untouched in Tamil.
