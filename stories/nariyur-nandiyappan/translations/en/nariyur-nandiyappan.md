# Nandiyappan of Nariyur

To the illustrious Nandiyappar, respectful greetings from Nariyappan, panchayat man of Nandiyur. I received the golden letter drawn by your sacred hand and was thrilled. Your decision is indeed worthy of praise by everyone endowed with political wisdom. As for you, you possess Kubera-like wealth that would make even diamonds and vaiduryam gems shining with the brilliance of a crore suns seem trivial. The price of a single ceremonial fan waved for you is equal to the price of the car possessed by the administrator here. All the people dwelling on earth long to behold the splendid spectacle of your durbar hall. Everywhere one looks in your city there is such dazzling brilliance that one wonders whether they are burning lamps or a cluster of stars fallen from the sky.

Such is the play of time: despite all this, the status that ought to have been yours has been stripped away. What of it? Can the sun be hidden in pitch darkness? Does the fragrance of the lotus vanish in muddy slime? Through the turn of time, who can remove you from the great hearts of the people who regard you as a god?

O leader of Nariyur! I have often thought that I should live in Nariyur under your stewardship as a

citizen! Divine favour did not grant me that. Today you have arrived in Nandiyur. You will surely have understood the distinctions of Nandiyur and the greatness of the administrators who govern here. I have enclosed with this letter a list of how many panchayat cases I have conducted here with great skill. Except in disputes arising between the administrators and the general public, I have delivered impartial and sound judgments in all other cases. There have been many unavoidable occasions when I had to stand on the administrators' side and oppose justice. Now, if you grant me this other temporary post of assenter, my plight will become very difficult. In old-time plays the same actor would take two roles—Madhavi first, Kannagi later. That was the age when men themselves played women's roles! There was no natural beauty; everything was artificial beauty!

They would arrange what had to be arranged, conceal what had to be concealed, apply stage paint, and appear on stage. Just so, my two offices too may become an unnatural pretence. In the play, Madhavi and Kannagi are women of two different characters, hostile to and denouncing one another. My panchayat role would be like that: it would be hostile to many matters approved by the assenter and would have to denounce

them. If the two posts keep circling back to me in turn like a game of sadugudu, situations will arise in which I must oppose my own decision and pass judgment.

My lord! You probably have not seen a film called *Parasakthi*. There is an amusing incident in it. A madman named Gunasekaran stands near a wayside burden-rest stone and performs some comic antics filled with meaning.

“Minister! Are all the people in our great city well?” he asks, imagining himself to be a king. A minister is needed to answer him, is he not? He himself comes down, stands there acting as the minister too, and replies, “Your Majesty! All the people are starving to death!”

For me to hold both the panchayat post and the assenter post would be just such a madman's farce. But after seeing your message, I reached a decision. I was already halfway on the administrators' side. Now I have no other course except to move entirely onto the administrators' side.

For your honour and status, this assenter post is like placing a flower where gold belongs! For me, though, it is a golden flower—a flower of fame! Reading aloud what the administrators write is certainly something you cannot bear. I accept it without

regarding it as a sacrifice I am making for you; I regard it instead as a great opportunity that has come to me. As you have assured in your letter, I trust you will observe the arrangement of one week in the assenter's post and nine weeks touring the country.

If you do so, it will be convenient for me too, along with my relatives and friends, to travel to various sacred kshetras and obtain divine grace for you as well. Finally, one thing: it appears that you too consider this an unnecessary post. Is this the only unnecessary post in Nandiyur? There are so many! Let this too remain as the eleventh among ten; should not people like me get to enjoy it?

Your grateful,

Nariyappan.
