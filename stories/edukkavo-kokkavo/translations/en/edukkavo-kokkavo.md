I cannot help thinking about the first speech I gave in the school debating contests that prepared me for public speaking. The very first time I stood before an assembly and raised my voice, the subject was “Friendship”! I spoke by quoting examples from the Bharatham. Karna and Duryodhana were two friends. One day Karna and Duryodhana's wife were playing chess. At that moment Duryodhana came there. Seeing her husband arrive, his wife rose from the game as a mark of respect. Karna, who had not noticed Duryodhana's arrival, thought that she intended to leave the game and,

when he pulled at her pearl necklace, the strand broke and the pearls scattered to the floor. Without the slightest suspicion, Duryodhana smiled, patted his friend and asked, “Shall I pick them up or string them?” He was asking whether he should gather the scattered pearls and string them together. Karna was overwhelmed by his friend's greatness of heart. For his friend, he met Sotharai on the battlefield, fought, and finally gave up his life. I narrated this episode beautifully from Villiputhurar's Bharatham and won tremendous support from the assembly. Even the teachers praised me for pouring out a rain of words in beautiful Tamil without hesitation or interruption. How could any of them have known the secret—that for three days and nights I had written the speech, memorised it, and practised delivering it to the pillars in my house? Only now does that become clear. Karna and Duryodhana possessed the great quality called “friendship” — but by seizing and pulling at another man's wife's necklace, Karna also displayed a deeply improper trait.
