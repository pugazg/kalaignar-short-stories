The poet Nappasalaiyar was a close friend of the great king Killi Valavan. He sings about that Killi Valavan.

Killi Valavan dies. The poet asks: how could he possibly have died? No one could kill him!

“Death that took Valavan,
the strong-shouldered one,
whose army crossed
the crowded battlefield!”

The poet asks how Kootruvan—Yama—could have taken the life of such a great king. Why should he not have been able to take it?

“Had he come as an enemy,
had he come in anger,
had he come against him,
there would have been no escape!”

Yama could not have taken Killi Valavan's life by opposing him, dealing with him directly, or fighting him. Then how could he have taken it?

“Like one who sings,
with joined hands praising,
he must have begged
and asked.”

Yama must have disguised himself as a poet, gone to Killi Valavan and asked him to “give” his life. The poet says that Killi Valavan, thinking Kootruvan was a poet, must have given him his life.

Nappasalaiyar's poem is an example of the kind of respect kings had for poets in those days.
