# The Tenali Raman Story

> **English translation of the verified Tamil archival reading.** Controlling source: 1987 collection `கலைஞர் சொன்ன குட்டிக் கதைகள்`, lower scan **37 / printed page 36 → scan 38 / printed page 37 → upper scan 39 / printed page 38**.

After M.G.R. assumed office as Chief Minister, I asked in the Legislative Assembly in my capacity as Leader of the Opposition: “As under the Kazhagam government, will farmers be given the bonus and 15 rupees toward transport expenses?” He said that more would be given than what we had given.

Two years have passed since he said that. Nothing was given during these two years, and now he says there will be 20 rupees more, a procurement centre, arrangements for the government to buy, and so on.

Last week Edmund said that there was no way for the government to provide the paddy. “To buy it like that, we

<!-- anthology scan: 38; printed page: 37 -->

have no money,” he said. But today M.G.R. has said, “I will buy the paddy.” What this is like is the story of Tenali Raman.

One day Tenali Raman met the king. “Tenali Rama, beautiful paintings must be prepared in the mansion beside our palace. I want to look at them and be pleased,” said the king.

“What great matter is this, king? I myself will paint the pictures. Give me ten thousand gold coins,” he said.

The king gave him ten thousand gold coins. Tenali Raman said the paintings would be ready in six months.

The man who took the ten thousand gold coins did not return. After six months he came back to the king.

“Are the paintings ready? May I see them?” asked the king. “You may,” said Tenali Raman, though not a single painting had been made until then.

“I shall come tomorrow morning to see the paintings,” said the king. Tenali Raman wondered how to satisfy him. He mixed cow dung in a pot, dipped a broom into it, and went on drawing streaks in many places on the palace

<!-- anthology scan: 39; printed page: 38; source span: upper -->

's white walls.

In the morning the king came. Tenali Raman took the king along. There were no paintings to be seen. There were only large streaks made with cow dung. “What are all these?” the king asked angrily.

“These are all horses' tails,” said Tenali Raman. “Where are the horses?” asked the king. Tenali Raman said they were on the other side of the wall.

Just as Tenali Raman showed the king horses' tails, M.G.R. is showing the farming people a higher price of 20 rupees. Tomorrow, if they ask, “Where are the horses?” just as Tenali Raman says, “They are behind the wall,” if they ask, “Where is the procurement centre?” he will say, “It will come after the election.”

Like the horses behind the wall, it is not going to come after the election.
