In Kailasa, Lord Siva was sleeping soundly. The snake around his neck had climbed down and was playing with the fawn. Nearby, Goddess Parvati lay in deep sleep, unaware even that the bull was licking her feet with its rough tongue. It seems that even the women of the celestial world would not know if thunder fell while they slept; otherwise, could Indra's son, in the form of a crow, have admired Sita Piratti's body while she slept? Beads of sweat had formed on Parvati's face. Parama Siva's body too lay motionless in sleep. Does not Maheswaran have five heads? He had removed four of them, wrapped them in deerskin, and put them away in a corner; otherwise sleeping would hardly have been convenient!

Deep sleep—silence—and, tearing through that silence, the heavy breathing of the divine couple.

One lock of hair on Siva's head moved a little faster. Following that movement, Ganga was walking along. Placing her soft, catlike feet with great ease upon Siva's head, she looked cautiously around and went somewhere.

She stopped beneath one of the locks, stretching her golden arms among the branches, waiting

for someone. Two long arms reached through the branches and covered her blue-black eyes.

Without thinking, Ganga's hands caught hold of those hands. The joining of their hands looked like a rose fixed upon an alvi stalk.

“Chandra!” The call sounded like the low-register hum of a bee stirred into rapture after drinking honey.

Chandra....yes, the Moon himself. That young man who lives upon Siva's head. They say one should not place fire beside cotton. Yet Parameswaran is the one who keeps a snake beside Murugan's peacock. A man who kept Ganga on his head might at least have kept Chandra at his feet; what can one say—‘poor man’, such was his fate!

“Ganga! My dear!”

“Chandra!.....O sallapa rupa!”

“My love!.....Why didn't you come yesterday?”

“Yesterday was my turn; today is Parvati's turn.”

“Speak softly....Siva may wake up.”

“Luckily....he has no eye on his head; even if he wakes, there is nothing to fear....”

“You are a tease, Ganga!”

“And what are you?”

“Ganga....! Do you like talking with that old man?”

“If I liked it....why would I go looking for Chandra....?”

“You speak in Tara's very tune.......She says the same sort of thing; her husband, with his beard and moustache, troubles her in his advanced age.”

“If women like Tara and Ganga end up with old men who have reached that age, Chandras like you will have a fine hunt.”

“Whatever the case, what we are doing is a sin....isn't it?”

“Chandra....! If people commit sin on earth, they cannot come to Kailasa. But we are already in Kailasa. We ought to discover whether there is any sin greater than this, don't you think? Are we the ones responsible for all this sin? Tara loved you. Fools said she betrayed her husband. They created a heart in Tara—desire within it—a longing to taste the joy of a happy life—and after creating all that....who asked them to call her a prostitute? They say your union with your guru's wife was a sin. The babbling of madmen. If relieving his wife's sorrow is service to the guru, is that not great service?”

“What did I do, after all! Bhagiratha called me from the sky. Seeing Bhagiratha weep, my body trembled. My body alone? My very heart trembled. I cannot describe that thirst of desire, Chandra....! Kamban would have to be born again; so far beyond all bounds had my feeling gone. A king of a country; an emperor possessed of every pleasure and luxury; I dreamed that by exchanging amorous delights with him....I might attain the fulfilment of this birth. But that helpless fellow....pushed me into the hands of this old man of Kailasa. He is old....and not merely old....a skeleton who wore his very body away for the wives of the sages of Darukavana. Is that all, Chandra!....Parvati is beside him too. There is a timetable for pleasure: one woman's turn each day. Can I bear that?....Can young women like me bear it?....Could it be done only by my..Parvati?....One should ask Brahma for that story. Chandra! You go to earth every day.....will you teach those people this one message?”

“What is it, Ganga?”

“Do not give a woman in marriage to an old man! Do not consent to a two-wife marriage! Will you tell them this?....Say it for my sake

....Say it so that women like me may not arise again.....?”

“So be it, my dear....come—let us go toward the nape. It will be dark there.”

Chandra and Ganga disappeared. A little while later, the Lord suddenly awoke. Beside him Parvati was still sleeping motionless. He touched his head. Only then did Ganga come running back in great haste and sit there.
