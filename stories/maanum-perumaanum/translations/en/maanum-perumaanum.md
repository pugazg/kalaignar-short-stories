The Prophet Muhammad is walking through a forest. A deer comes running, pursued by a hunter. The circumstances are these: in a herd full of deer there are a stag, a doe, and a fawn only three days old. The hunter chases all three in order to catch them. The stag and the fawn escape with the herd, while the doe

<!-- source scan 70; printed page 68 -->

is trapped alone. He chases it and catches it. As he drags it away, the Prophet comes toward them. Looking at the hunter, the doe pleads, “Let me go.” It begs, “Separated from me, the stag will be sunk in inconsolable sorrow. Not only that. I must give milk to my fawn, which is not even three days old. Hunter, let me go.” “I cannot,” the hunter refuses. The Prophet intervenes. “Have you no compassion, my man? The deer is pleading that it must give milk to its fawn. Let it go,” he says. The hunter says he cannot.

Then the Prophet says, “Release that deer. It will go and return to you. It will give milk to its fawn and come back.” The hunter refuses to believe him. The Prophet says, “If you do not believe it, until that deer returns to you I will remain beside you as its surety—as its guarantor.”

After that the hunter sends the deer away on the condition that it must give milk to its fawn and return immediately. The doe runs in search of its fawn. Even as it runs, through the feeling of love that it is going to feed its young, milk wells up and flows all along the way. It

is like the light of the white moon, Umaru Pulavar describes.

After feeding its fawn, the doe takes leave of the stag. “I must go back to the hunter. The Prophet Muhammad is there as guarantor for me,” it says. All the other deer try to stop it from going. Then the doe says, “Rather than live after failing to keep the promise I gave, it would be better for me to be struck down by a tiger and die.”

Such is the integrity of that deer. This is the deer pointed out by Umaru Pulavar in the Seerapuranam. It returns to the hunter. Seeing the deer's integrity, the hunter says, “Live on,” blesses it and lets it go; he also bows to the Prophet Muhammad. Thus that episode of the puranam ends.
