# Kadalai Thoorppathu Miga Elithu

<!-- source scan 11; printed page 10 -->

One day I was walking along the seashore. A strange figure was moving toward the sea. I was overcome with wonder! The figure was neither like a man nor like a woman. It did not seem to belong to the bird kind. Nor did it look like an animal. Driven by a desire to solve this riddle somehow, I walked quickly toward the figure. As I drew nearer and nearer, I was able to see its whole appearance.

Its body was shaped like a banyan leaf. There was no separate head. In the very banyan-leaf shape were the organs of a face—eyes, ears, nose and mouth. Two vine-like legs and, likewise, two vine-like arms.

I stood astonished. The figure looked at me, but did not stop. Wondering what it would do, I quietly

stood right there. Suddenly the figure stretched out its hands and scooped up sand from the shore. It carried the sand to the sea and threw it in. Again and again it did the same thing. At first I thought it was merely playing by scooping sand, but the figure's action became a great puzzle to me. It scooped sand. It threw it into the sea. It paused, a little tired. Then again it scooped sand and scattered it into the sea.

Summoning courage, I went a little closer. The figure looked me up and down and bent to scoop up sand.

“Who are you?”

That was my question.

“Don't you know me? I am the heart!” said the figure.

“Oh! The heart? Have you sprouted arms, legs, eyes and a nose? All right, all right—whose heart are you? Male or female?”

“Can you not tell from my vine-like legs that I am a woman's heart?”

Everything became clear to me. I understood plainly that a woman's heart, because of some anguish, had come out like this. I continued questioning it.

“O woman's heart! Why have you alone come here by yourself?”

“This is my lady's command!”

“Your lady sent you alone? Fine, she sent you! But was it also your lady's command that you take this sand and throw it into the sea?”

“Yes!”

“Why this useless work—for you and for your lady?”

“My lady called me. ‘O my heart! There is no use telling someone who has no love for you about your great suffering and the pain of separation. An easier task than that is to scoop up sand and fill the deep sea! So do that work,’ she said. That is what I am doing now.”

When I heard this answer, my head spun. Alas! What kind of grief must that lady's be! It seems filling the sea is easier than telling her anguish to her lover and making him love her.

I woke up.

I understood that it had been a dream.
