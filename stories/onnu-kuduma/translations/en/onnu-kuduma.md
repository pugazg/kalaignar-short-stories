Kalaivaanar and Udumalai Narayana Kavi together brought about a revolution in ideas in Tamil Nadu.

<!-- source scan 34; printed page 32 -->

In the film ‘Uthama Puthiran’ of those days, Kalaivaanar appears as an Iyer. The woman he loves, however, is an Adi Dravidar woman carrying a bundle of grass! This leads to many disputes, and in the end the film depicts circumstances in which that very woman is married off to a high-caste man!

Today it is easy to talk about social reform; but in those days it was Kalaivaanar who dared to include scenes like these!

Here is another example of his wonderful comedy.

The father outside! The daughter in the room! And between them, the young man (Kalaivaanar) who wants to reach her!

“I’ll go inside and get a mango from your daughter,” says Kalaivaanar.

“All right,” says the father, and then tells his daughter inside, “Here, dear, he’ll come in—give him one.”

Kalaivaanar goes inside and asks the young woman for a kiss! She refuses!

Kalaivaanar immediately peeks outside and says, “Your daughter says she won’t give it.”

The father says, “Give him one, dear!” and Kalaivaanar gets the kiss.

What kind of comedy is that? Comedy like this could arise only in Kalaivaanar’s imagination.
