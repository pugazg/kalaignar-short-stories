# The Mutilated-Nose Man

> **English translation of the verified Tamil archival reading.** Controlling source: 1987 collection `கலைஞர் சொன்ன குட்டிக் கதைகள்`, lower scan **12 → upper 13**. The verified Tamil remains authoritative.

<!-- anthology scan: 12; printed page: 11; lower shared span -->

A man whose nose was deformed was mocked by everyone. One day, standing in the market street, he cut off his own deformed nose. The man who had cut it off shouted, “Ah! I can see God! I can see God!” Those nearby asked, “Can you see God? How?” He replied, “If you too cut off your nose, you too will see God.” Hearing this, one man cut off his own nose. But he did not see God. He thought that if he went and stood there with his nose cut off, people in the town would—

<!-- anthology scan: 13; printed page: 12; upper shared span -->

—mock him. So, wanting others to cut off their noses too, he said, “Yes! I can see God!” Believing him, everyone cut off their noses. The old story says that everyone in that town became noseless mutilated people.

Likewise—someone said that a magician had told M.G.R. that a danger hung over him and that therefore he should wear a black shirt. Because wearing a black shirt by himself alone would become an object of ridicule, M.G.R. ordered everyone in his party to wear a black shirt. This is reported as reliable information.

Accordingly, listening to the magician, he wore a black shirt for two mandalams—that is, ninety-six days—and then took it off. His followers too wore black shirts and then took them off. That was the full extent of the interest and concern they showed for Tamils!

## Translation note

- The title `மூளி மூக்குக்காரன்` is rendered conservatively as **The Mutilated-Nose Man**; no modernized title has replaced the verified Tamil.
- `கண்டம்` is rendered only as “a danger”; no astrological explanation has been imported.
- The scan-12/scan-13 split is retained at the source break between `ஊரில்` and `கேலி பேசுவார்களே`.
