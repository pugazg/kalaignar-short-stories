“Victory to Sangiliyananda Swami!”

“Victory to Sangiliyananda Swami! Victory! Victory! Victory!”

The devotees leapt about; they danced in ecstasy; they sang songs of supreme bliss.

“Praise to Ashtama Siddhupuri Jayanē! Praise to the father who curbs the insolence of the wicked! Praise to our visible god who dispels our troubles! Praise! Praise!!”

The swami’s disciple, Sambandham, sang this hymn with melting emotion. Sambandham’s facial contortions...the bodily gestures he deliberately summoned...gave fresh lustre to the song of praise and plunged the crowd of devotees into rapture.

A single mighty cry of ‘Namap-Parvati Padē’; then sudden silence. Chinnapannai Mudaliar touched the swami’s feet in worship and showered flowers upon them. The devotees too...following the landlord, performed worship at his feet...and stood offering flowers...coins...money. With the murmured chant ‘Om Sankara Siva’, Sangilichami blessed those who rose after bowing to him.

<!-- source scan 58; printed page 49 -->

The devotees were of many kinds; the boons they sought were of many kinds too.

“I must win my court case.”

“My rheumatic illness must be cured.”

“No child....I need grace.”

“I have been robbed; show me the thief!”

For everything there was one medicine......sacred ash. The swami gave it, and the multitudes of devotees received it in a veritable flood of devotion.

‘Sambandham!’ said the swami.

‘Swami!’ cried the disciple, collapsing before him. By then Chinnapannai Mudaliar stood with folded hands and covered mouth, sharpening his ears to catch every word.

“Sankaran commands that today’s grace is enough,” came Sangilichami’s sweet declaration.

“Exactly so, my lord!” said Sambandham.

“The swami is going to repose....all the people may leave,” said Mudaliar.

“Look at him!....He looks as though he has jumped straight down from the celestial world.”

“Ruddy red....just like Lord Siva! Ah! What grace! What grace! With that saffron robe....and that face flowing with compassion, it is as though the Lord of Kailasa himself has come!”

“When he looks toward the women and talks, his whole mouth is smiling, girl! Ah...he is plump and glowing like a lemon. A divine birth, girl, a divine birth!”

“My own man is there at home—could he speak even four words this beautifully?”

“Ghosts....even afflictions that never go away are cured by this man’s sacred ash, da.”

<!-- source scan 59; printed page 50 -->

“Is he an ordinary man? He saved a thousand people from danger!”

“What danger was that?”

“Don’t you know?....Do you know why he is called Sangilichami?”

“He worships Sangili Karuppan.”

“You fool, not that. One day the Madras Mail....was racing along at tremendous speed, like a mail train.”

“To which town?”

“To Madras, appa.”

“Hmm; go on?”

“Our swami was in it too. The swami....was sitting there as usual....”

“Did he vanish?”

“No, appa....he grabbed the emergency-warning chain in the train and pulled it.”

“The train must have stopped at once!”

“Yes; as soon as it stopped....the guard came and asked, ‘Who stopped the train?’”

“What did the sadhu say?”

“‘I did, appa! There is danger on the track, appa! Go and look at once, appa’—that is what the swami said.”

“Ahaha....what power! What power!”

“When everyone went and inspected the track....they found the rails cut....a plot had been made to overturn the train.”

“Good heavens!”

“Immediately all the people....fell full-length at the swami’s feet, saying, ‘God who saved us!’ and embraced him, it seems.”

<!-- source scan 60; printed page 51 -->

“Tch! Tch!..God incarnate himself! Somehow, in this Kali age, we have been blessed enough to see him.”

“That is how he got the name Sangilichami! Our Chinnapannai Mudaliar....was on that same train. From then on he developed....a fascination for the swami.”

“Oh....so that’s why....Mudaliar is making such a grand show!....Otherwise....would that miser ever spend money like this?”

Upstairs in Chinnapannai’s house, Sangiliyananda Swami reclined on a silk mattress....Sambandham massaged his feet....and the landlord worked the ‘pankha’. On another mattress lay the landlord’s little dog.

‘Om....Sankara Siva’—these were the words that emerged from the swami’s mouth once every quarter-hour!...

“What, Mudaliar!...Paramesan commands that our journey must take place soon.”

“A command from the Lord?....Then we must certainly leave”—Sambandham trembled with fervour.

Mudaliar stood up. “Swami, by tomorrow morning all of it will have come. After that, if we begin the journey to that place....” Before he could finish,

“No....no; our Lord will be angry with the swami. For a trivial matter, there must not arise ill feeling between the swami and the manifest Parameswaran.”

Sambandham danced about crying “thaththom”; the swami stilled him with a hand and said, “Sambandham!...Wait! We must not deceive Mudaliar, who shows us such affection. Very well...I shall ask Sankaran for one day’s extension. Mudaliar! How much has come in so far?”

A glow rose on Mudaliar’s face.

<!-- source scan 61; printed page 52 -->

“Swami....only a thousand rupees’ weight of silver has come. Another thousand rupees’ weight will come in the morning.”

“Good. Two thousand rupees’ weight in all. If we turn all of it into gold...that will be enough, won’t it?”

Mudaliar twisted his face into a grin, nodded, and bared his teeth.

A few wrinkles flashed on the swami’s forehead and vanished.

“Sambandham!”

“Swami!”

“After tomorrow morning’s puja is over, no one must come upstairs.”

“Command accepted.”

“You too.”

“So be it.”

“I must remain alone all night and perform Siva puja.”

“So be it, Swami.”

“Mudaliar.....”

“Swami.....”

“Understood?”

“Ahaha...”

“After the night puja is complete—and the next day dawns....you must come upstairs....your wish will have been fulfilled....what you asked for was gold, was it not?”

“Yes....and if it can be made into something still more valuable......”

“Do not worry....it can even be made into a slab of diamond, depending on how Siva’s grace manifests....!”

<!-- source scan 62; printed page 53 -->

Mudaliar held out a bowl of milk to the swami. The disciple Sambandham took it and held it to the swami’s lips....

The next morning, after the usual worship was completed...the silver ingots too arrived. The swami gazed at those slabs of silver to his heart’s content and declared, “As a mūḍātmā becomes a jñānātmā—as an atheist becomes a believer—O silver! You are going to become gold!”

As the swami said this, Sambandham nodded....and the sight of Mudaliar’s face and heart blooming was exceedingly delightful.

The swami sat down to perform puja. Clouds of benzoin smoke, the scent of incense.........the fragrance of ground sandal......the cool perfume of jasmine garlands.....

“The swami has created a whole heavenly world upstairs,” said Mudaliar.

“Only if there is a heavenly world will Sokkanathan come,” said Sambandham.

All night Mudaliar dreamed only one dream after another: the brilliance of the silver ingots transformed into gold; breaking up those gold bars and going elsewhere to trade them; after selling them for a good price.....becoming a great Kubera; after becoming Kubera, buying the great local estate at auction. He tossed about in these dreams. Then, in a terrifying dream, he was caught by the police where he had gone to trade the gold....Mudaliar sprang awake in panic. He rubbed his eyes as though unable to believe them. Dawn had come. Eager to see the gold, he called Sambandham and ran upstairs.

The smell of benzoin lingered everywhere....as though offering Mudaliar an enthusiastic welcome. Entering the upper floor, Mudaliar shouted, ‘Swami...Swami..’ That was all; he fainted.

<!-- source scan 63; printed page 54 -->

Sambandham lifted Mudaliar and began reviving him. A heavy rope hanging from the upper floor toward the garden swayed in the breeze. Mudaliar recovered from his faint and stared at Sambandham. His look reflected the grief: ‘Hey, Sambandham! Two thousand rupees’ weight of silver is gone!’

Sambandham’s answering look seemed to say, “Great greed, great loss.”

“Sambandham! Hasn’t my story ended like the tale of the Brahmin woman whose dried fish was stolen?”

“Yes....it would be shameful if this became known outside.”

“I’ll have to go to jail, appa.”

“Hmm....that wretch has done this to us!”

“Sambandham....were you also an accomplice of this conspirator?”

“Mudaliar!...forgive me. I too was indeed his accomplice. That great sinner deceived me as well.”

“You acted like supreme devotees!”

“If we had not acted, would you have believed us?”

“You sinner...from the moment I saw him on the train, Saturn seized me.”

“Not Saturn! Proper greed seized you. Mudaliar! Are not dangers born from desire?”

“Enough of philosophy, appa. It was listening to your philosophy that brought all this calamity.”

“I will speak no more philosophy. I will find a way to destroy that rogue.”

“My property has gone into the sea—will it ever come ashore again?”

“Sambandham’s ship will scrape up every last splinter and bring it back. Do not be troubled, Mudaliar!”

<!-- source scan 64; printed page 55 -->

“My silver ingots, appa...my silver ingots!”

“I will return them as diamond ingots. Do not grieve.”

“Why...do you want a gold bar for yourself too?”

“Do not judge me wrongly...Mudaliar!...If you hear my story, you will pity me.”

“Tell me whatever damned thing it is and be done with it.”

“I am an ordinary man.”

“That much is obvious...”

“Sangilichami too is an ordinary man...”

“Who said he wasn’t?”

“He saved the people from that railway disaster....”

“That is exactly what fooled me too!”

“Not only you—the whole world was fooled!”

“A conjurer; somehow he cast a spell and bewitched us.”

“Not magic...a magnificent trick.”

“A trick?”

“Yes. Do you know who removed the railway track?”

“Was it that brute?”

“No...it was I, Mudaliar! I struggled all night and broke the track. He pulled the chain and became a swami.”

“You certainly found an extraordinary way to make a living.”

“An extraordinary way indeed! Yet that treacherous fellow has thrown dust into my mouth too!”

“Sambandham...there is no time for talk. We must take revenge on him. What is your plan?”

<!-- source scan 65; printed page 56 -->

“Mudaliar...an excellent plan. Shut the upstairs door this very moment.”

“Why?”

“People will come and ask...‘Where is the swami?’ We shall say the swami is upstairs in yogic bliss, speaking with God, and that the door cannot be opened.”

“Will doing this bring back my silver?”

“I will make it possible for you to build a house out of silver itself. First you must do what I say.”

“All right...I’ll listen.”

“Good! I am leaving at once.”

“Where are you going, Sambandham?”

“I myself do not know where I am going. Within a week a parcel will come for you. Without anyone knowing, take it upstairs, break it open, and look.”

“A parcel containing the silver ingots I lost?”

“Yes. I will return. Until the parcel arrives, the upstairs room must not be opened.”

Sambandham disappeared. Lines of thought scored Mudaliar’s face! The news spread all over town that Sangilichami was in nishta.

“The swami is speaking with God.”

“Perhaps he may even go off to heaven!”

“Is he talking with Siva? With Vishnu? Who knows?”

“He is a worshipper of Sakti, appa! He must be talking with the Goddess.”

One day a box arrived for Chinnapannai Mudaliar as a railway parcel.

Mudaliar eagerly ordered the box to be unloaded at his house.

<!-- source scan 66; printed page 57 -->

The words ‘Perfumed Goods’ had been painted on the box.

Mudaliar lay longing for night to come.

Within a few hours night arrived.

All the servants were sent home early that day.

A rich ‘kamakam’ fragrance kept wafting from the parcel.

Suddenly Sambandham too arrived.

“Sambandham! It is all silver ingots, isn’t it!!” Mudaliar asked eagerly.

Nodding, Sambandham said, “Yes, lift it,” in a commanding tone.

The two lifted the parcel. Mudaliar, who had never known what labour meant...must somehow have learned it from the weight of that box. Sweat dripped and dripped...he panted for breath...and with an ‘appaadaa’ they lowered the parcel upstairs. “Now you know the hardship of work,” Sambandham mocked.

“The hardship of silver, appa!” Mudaliar muttered.

Sambandham began breaking open the parcel. Unable to bear the suspense, Mudaliar trembled: “Appa Sambandham! It is silver, isn’t it?”

“Yes”—Sambandham broke open the box. Mudaliar fainted and fell. Then he came to. Sambandham clapped a hand over his mouth to stop him from screaming.

Inside the box lay a human form curled up like a worm!

<!-- source scan 67; printed page 58 -->

Yes—it was Sangilichami’s corpse!

“What is this, Sambandham?” Mudaliar shook and trembled. “Not only is my silver gone—you have made me into a murderer too....Ayyo!”

“No...we are not murderers; we are servants of God, devotees among devotees.”

As he said this, Sambandham took Sangilichami’s corpse and stretched and folded it into a pose as though he were sitting in nishta. He lit the lamp beside it. Then he pulled Mudaliar outside and shut the upstairs door. Mudaliar stared wildly.

Sambandham said softly:—“Mudaliar! Do not fear! You lost two thousand rupees’ weight of silver. I shall give you twenty thousand rupees’ weight.”

Time blossomed. Word went out that the swami’s nishta was about to end. They were going to open the door. The swami had supposedly appeared in Mudaliar’s dream and ordered the door opened. Following this publicity, a huge crowd gathered before Mudaliar’s house! The door was opened. Everyone went upstairs. Sambandham wailed aloud. Mudaliar too wept...the two performed the drama expertly.

A letter was found there. It read,

Sambandham read it aloud.

one thousand rupees! The following year: Sangiliyananda Swami Guru Puja!

A grand mutt for the swami; Sambandham the swami of the mutt! Mudaliar the owner of the mutt! Loss: two thousand rupees’ weight of silver ingots!

But...profit: twenty thousand rupees’ weight of silver! And a profit that keeps growing!....As long as foolishness remains among the people, that profit will not diminish.
