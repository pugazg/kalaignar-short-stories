## Embedded-story and sensitive-content review

- the frame is fundamentally about alleged literary theft; the embedded Amirthamathi story functions as the speaker’s analogy and remains subordinate to that frame;
- the source itself identifies the *Yasodhara Kaviyam* as a Jain work among the five minor epics and as concerning the king of Avanti; no outside literary history was added;
- Amirthamathi’s attraction to the elephant keeper, his violence toward her, her adultery and poisoning of Yasodharan are translated to the source’s degree of explicitness without added sexual or violent detail;
- the speaker’s curses against the alleged literary thieves are retained as his speech, not presented as an external factual judgment;
- the story deliberately withholds the thieves’ identity at the end, and the English does the same.
