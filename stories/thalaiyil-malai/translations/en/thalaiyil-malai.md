“I will carry a mountain on my head and go in procession throughout the country! That is my first scheme! I will bend the sky into a bow, twist the earth into a rope for the string suited to that bow, and show it to you! That is my second scheme! I have many more marvellous schemes one after another. I will demonstrate them

later before the people of this country.”

Thus, in the midst of a public assembly, a young man slapped his shoulders and issued his challenge. The people listened to his words open-mouthed and were spellbound. Word that such a young man was roaming about speaking so boldly reached the king’s ears as well. The king ordered that he be brought to the royal audience hall. The youth entered the hall with a swaggering gait. Without having him seated, the king asked whether the schemes he had announced to the people were truly possible!

“There is nothing in this world that cannot be done, Your Majesty! I will certainly carry out the schemes I have announced,” the young man proclaimed, standing erect.

“If so, valiant young man, carry out your first scheme before me! Are you ready?”

To this question, overflowing with the king’s eagerness, he replied with great calm.

“O king of kings? Does not every undertaking require certain facilities and opportunities? I will certainly carry out the deeds I have spoken of before the people of the country and in your august presence. But one thing! I have travelled for many days and am exhausted. If it pleases you, as a guest of the palace for a month or two,

<!-- source scan 13; printed page 11 -->

I ask that you accept me. Once my body has gained strength, I will demonstrate my first scheme—the feat of carrying a mountain on my head,” he said.

The king gladly accepted his request. “Why merely a month or two? Take a full three months! During those three months, every facility you require will be provided to you in the palace,” he said, giving him an encouraging pat.

The people counted the days, waiting to see the young man’s heroic feats.

He had a life of luxury in the palace! Foods that gave strength to the body! Drinks that brought delight to the heart! Whatever he asked for, he received.

The king kept telling his ministers that, if the young man actually carried out the schemes he had announced, he intended to appoint him commander-in-chief of his army.

Three months came to an end. The day was announced on which the first scheme—the plan to carry a mountain on his head and walk—was to be fulfilled. The whole country gathered at the foot of the mountain. The king, the ministers and the palace officials sat in their respective places. The young man arrived in a brightly coloured chariot

<!-- source scan 14; printed page 12 -->

and stepped down. His body shone with new radiance and strength. The effect of three months of palace living was plainly visible in his physique.

The king and the people welcomed him with applause. Drums thundered. Musical instruments sounded. The young man raised both his hands. Every sound ceased and silence settled. He walked toward the foot of the mountain. Every eye watched without blinking, fixed on the event about to unfold.

“I am ready to carry the mountain on my head and walk!” he shouted. A single roar of joy rose up!

But what happened? He did not lift the mountain. Again and again he kept saying only, “I am ready to carry the mountain on my head and walk.” Tired of hearing those same words over and over, everyone moved closer to him.

“Why do you keep saying the same thing? Show us by doing it!” the king said in a loud voice.

“My king, I said that I would walk carrying the mountain on my head. Do not forget that. All of you join together right now and lift this mountain onto my head. Then see whether I walk with it across the

<!-- source scan 15; printed page 13 -->

whole country or not!”

Thus, with great humility, the young man offered his explanation.

“Arrest him!” the king roared like a leopard.

“From now on, do whatever you wish with me. But one thing! I have fulfilled the vow I made to my friends and to my wife. I vowed that I would return after spending at least a month in the palace as an important guest. That vow has now been fulfilled.”

With a smile blossoming on his face, the young man answered without fear.

“Hey! If that was the sort of desire you had, you could have said something you were actually capable of doing and, as the reward for it, eaten your fill at the palace!” the king rebuked him.

“If a man says only what he can actually do, who respects him for saying it? Your Majesty! It is only when one says, ‘I will accomplish wonders,’ that adulation, affectionate welcomes and all the rest come one’s way!”

Hearing the young man’s reply, the king sprang up in fury.

“Oh! So that is why you deceived us?” he roared.

“O king of kings! When I first entered this country, I asked whether the Tirukkural was in use here! When I heard that even the king did not know the Tirukkural, I boldly set about my attempt. If you had read the Tirukkural, neither you nor the people of your country would have accepted my claim.”

This was how the young man answered.

“What is there in that Kural, then?”

That was the king’s question!

The Kural quoted by the young man was this:

“To speak is easy for anyone; rare indeed
is acting in the manner one has spoken.”

It is very easy for anyone merely to say that he will accomplish a deed! But to complete the deed in the manner one has spoken is not easy: it is difficult—that is the meaning of this Kural.
