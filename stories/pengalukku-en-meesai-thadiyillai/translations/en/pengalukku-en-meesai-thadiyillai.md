# Pengalukku En - Meesai Thaadiyillai?

A story in the *Agananuru Purana*.

There was a rishi in the world of the gods. That rishi wished to tour the earthly world. Thinking that travelling alone would not be lively, he invited his wife to come with him.

<!-- source scan 7; printed page 6 -->

His wife said, “We have a daughter who has come of age. What would happen if we left her alone in the world of the gods and went away? So I will not come.”

“No, no. You too must come. We can leave our grown daughter in a safe place and go,” he said.

They wondered with whom they could leave the young woman.

Could they leave her with Siva? At once they discussed it among themselves. He already has Parvati beside him and Ganga on his head! As though those two were not enough, there is also the story that he raped all the rishis' wives in the Daruka forest. How could they trust him and leave their young girl with him?

Because this question arose, they dismissed Siva.

Could they leave her with Vishnu? they wondered.

As though Bama and Rukmini were not enough, he had sported with thousands of gopika women and even with Radha, another man's wife! So a doubt arose: how could they leave her with him? They dismissed him too.

Then, could they leave her with Indra? The wife immediately asked, “Very good—go ahead! With him? He raped Ahalya, a rishi's wife, and was subjected to such fierce wrath that his whole body bore a thousand eyes—sores! With him?”

<!-- source scan 8; printed page 7 -->

At last they reached a decision. They could leave her with Yama. He was Dharmaraja, a good man. With that decision, the rishi and his wife entrusted the young woman to Yama and left for the earthly world.

Once Yama looked at the woman in his palace. Was he not Dharmaraja? Even he was struck by temptation.

So Yama revealed his desire to the woman. And because the one expressing desire was Yama himself, she consented!

A relationship sprang up between them. Amid this relationship Yama had a worry: what if anyone came to know what they were doing? So during the daytime he would put the woman into his stomach and swallow her; only at night would he go to his bedchamber and spit her out. Then the two would enjoy themselves in the bedchamber. Thus, day after day, the love scene in Yama's world continued.

One day Yama went to a stream to bathe. While bathing, the stomach ought not to be heavy and sluggish, should it? So he spat the woman out into a crystal pavilion nearby, seated her there, and said, “Stay right here. I will bathe and return.” Then he went to bathe.

At that very time Agni Bhagavan came that way. He saw the maiden sitting in the crystal pavilion—

<!-- source scan 9; printed page 8 -->

and immediately fell in love with her. She too consented.

While the two were enjoying themselves in that crystal pavilion, Yama returned from his bath along that way. Afraid that it would be dangerous if he saw them, the young woman, in order to deceive Yama, lifted Agni Bhagavan, put him into her own stomach and swallowed him.

Yama, knowing nothing of any of this—poor fellow—lifted her as usual and swallowed her into his own stomach.

Vayu Bhagavan, who came searching for Agni Bhagavan, could not find his friend and began searching the whole world.

Keep this in mind now.

Inside Yama's stomach was the young woman! Inside the young woman's stomach was Agni Bhagavan!

When daytime came, thinking she should not simply sit idle, the young woman, while still inside Yama's stomach, spat out Agni Bhagavan from her own stomach and turned Yama's stomach itself into a bedchamber for the whole day!

Then at night, as usual, Yama would spit her out and stay with her. This became the routine.

Meanwhile Vayu Bhagavan searched the whole world. Agni could not be found. Can the world function without Agni? Would there be fire to light a cigarette? Would flame rise in the firewood to cook on the stove?

<!-- source scan 10; printed page 9 -->

And so the whole world cried out because Agni could not be found.

At that time the rishi and his wife, who had gone to tour the world, returned to the world of the gods. They could not find their daughter. They understood nothing. They lodged a complaint.

By then Vayu Bhagavan had secretly learned all these matters. Wanting to expose them, he arranged a feast for the gods and invited Siva, Vishnu, Brahma and Yama as special guests, along with the other gods.

Everyone came. The parents too were present.

Vayu Bhagavan ordered that three leaves be laid only for Yama.

Siva asked, “Why three leaves only for him? Are you insulting all of us?”

“No, no, wait a little,” Vayu said. Then, looking at Yama, he said, “Yama! Spit out the woman who is inside your stomach!” Unable to say anything, Yama spat the woman out.

The woman came out and sat at another leaf.

Two leaves—it became a double leaf.

“All right, what is the third leaf for?” Yama asked angrily. Vayu Bhagavan looked at the woman and said, “Golden treasure of chastity! Please send out from your stomach my dearest

<!-- source scan 11; printed page 10 -->

friend, Agni Bhagavan!”

At once she trembled and spat out Agni Bhagavan from her stomach. As Agni Bhagavan emerged, in that trembling moment the beard and moustache on the woman's face were singed away.

Until then, according to the story, women in the world had beards and moustaches on their faces just like men.

When Agni Bhagavan came out, the beard and moustache were singed away, were they not? From that time onward, all women born in the world are born without beard or moustache.
