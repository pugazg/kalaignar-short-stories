# The Lineage of the Kings of Lanka

<!-- source scan 25; printed page 24 -->

...In the Bengal country within India there was a kingdom. In that kingdom there was a princess. Her name was Subadevi. She was a girl who would not submit to her household. She was extravagant and considered unfit for family life. They tried to reform her. There was no way to make her change. Angered that they were trying to correct her, she left the palace. She walked outside, travelling many *kal* in distance. On the way she saw a rough man named Singan. This Singan was a man who beat people to death and ate them. He saw the woman. Because she was beautiful, he did not feel like killing her. He wanted to marry her. She too consented. She had no other way; refusal meant death. Having married her, the brute called Singan took Subadevi into a great cave and shut her inside. He sealed the mouth of the cave with a huge boulder. His life was to go out, return in the evening, remove the stone, enter the cave and enjoy himself. Two children were born to them in that cave. The boy was named Singabahu and the girl Singavalli. They grew up.

When the boy had grown, he asked his mother, “Why does Father shut us in here and go away somewhere like this? What is the mystery?” His mother told him all the truth.

“How long are we to remain shut in here, Mother? I too have my father's strength. Come with me,” he said.

<!-- source scan 26; printed page 25 -->

Taking his mother on one shoulder and his sister on the other, and possessing his father's strength, he pulled and pushed aside the great stone door with his hands, escaped from there and went out.

Singan returned from outside. His wife was gone. His children were gone. He searched for them and wandered about, but could not find them. Then he went toward a country called ‘Lala’. In his fury at not finding them, he beat and ate every person he encountered.

There was great turmoil in Lala. The people announced that the whole country would be given as a reward to whoever captured this brute Singan. Singan's own son Singabahu killed him, carried his father's head in his hand, displayed it in the hall, and obtained the right to rule the country of Lala as the reward for his father's head.

Would not a man who had obtained the right to rule a country need a queen? So that this right would not pass to anyone else, he made his own sister his wife. Singabahu the elder brother and Singavalli his sister had not one or two, but sixteen pairs of twins. One child among those sixteen pairs was Vijayan. Vijayan gathered seven hundred companions, preyed upon the whole town and engaged in vile acts. Seeing Vijayan involved in such disgraceful conduct, his father Singabahu put Vijayan and his seven hundred companions on a ship and sent them away, saying, “Go get lost somewhere.”

<!-- source scan 27; printed page 26 -->

The ship wandered at sea for many days and finally reached an island. That island was Lanka. Vijayan crowned himself king of the island of Lanka; the Sinhalese are his lineage.

...This is the historical note written in the history book of Lanka, a fourth-standard book prescribed for Tamil students...
