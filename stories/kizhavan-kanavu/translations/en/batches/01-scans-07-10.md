## “The Old Man's Dream”

*(An imagined narrative)*

... Hey; will you give it? Must I kick you?

— Kick me, then—let's see!

... You—may you go to ruin; give it and be done with it, boy.

— If this curse were going to come true, Lord Krishna would have died long ago!

... Is all this some game?

— Yes: Duplicate Krishna Leela.

In the Puranas! Krishna Leela is one episode. Krishna, moving amid the revelry of the Gopika women; the **Vastrabharana scene** from that supremely holy tale in which he stole the clothes of maidens who were bathing and climbed a tree—pictures of it are engraved in homes! Lenin, having seen this scene in the bhajan hall, staged a Kali-Yuga Krishna Leela of his own. One day, by the pond! With the resolve that many women who regarded Krishna himself as god, and his obscene antics themselves as divine plays, would gather there, Lenin began this revolution.

**Not in Russia!—in Tamil Nadu!!**

Lenin is the dear son of Vipulanandar. Whenever Vipulanandar and Mallika became absorbed in talk of reform, Lenin's ears too would incline in that direction.

<!-- source scan 8; printed page 4 -->

“Contradiction from start to finish.”

“I cannot even tell what comes first and what comes after.”

“One history in Valmiki's Ramayana.”

“One concoction in Kamba Ramayanam.”

“The Periya Puranam in one fashion.”

“The Bhagavatam its opposite.”

“Even in an age when the truth that the world is round is known, people still live believing the tale that the earth was rolled up like a mat!”

“Even the **Puranarthika Iyer** who teaches geography lessons at school keeps that very tale as a convenience for life.”

“Tamils will not understand any of this so long as they have lent away their self-respect!”

“Even for men and women to come together, the Aryan's favour is needed, isn't it?”

“Why on earth must these falsehoods lie about in Puranic form?”

“All of it was done to make life overflow with prosperity!”

“How many avenues, just so one section may eat and make merry? God, religion, shastra, custom, fate, stories of every kind; good heavens....!”

“When the physician, after setting up all these devices perversely in life, sings, ‘Do not trust life’—that is when laughter makes the belly burst.”

<!-- source scan 9; printed page 5 -->

“‘False! An air-filled bag! A bubble! Of use to no one!’—the homeland of these words is advancing in self-defence. Is that all, Mallika! Those of a fallen disposition do not understand the meaning of the life that stretches open before them. Those who wander about lamenting, ‘False, false’—if the bee swarming over a flower thought the flower false, would it hover through the flower garden? Youth that scatters and strings together sweet pearls surely takes the mother to be real. Does a woman live without trusting the husband who gives her caresses?”

“Will the ochre-robed folk who wander about saying, ‘This body itself is false!’ answer these questions?”

“Whatever you ask, fate is the mantra they seize upon.”

“Fate, fate! This band, boasting that simplicity and honesty mean poverty, has reached the highest pitch! The handrail of the staircase called religion! A great fortress that protects one's livelihood!”

“Open the door of your eyelids—the lightning eyes flash forth! A dawn of reason that makes one blush! Will you not see it, Mallika!”

“Ah, Vaidika orthodoxy, keep your distance! Even my empty talk is only a way of passing the time, my love!”

While Vipulanandar and Mallika were entwined in this delightful conversation—

<!-- source scan 10; printed page 6 -->

Lenin would be thinking about the future. Truly, the name Lenin suited him. A volcano of revolution! A surging sea! A leaping bull! A throbbing warrior! Lenin was the very mould of these descriptions!

Many years earlier in Russia, Lenin had confronted on the stage of death the politics of the wicked Tsar—politics that relished Rasputin's cruelty, ruled through injustice, kissed capitalist arrogance, seated religious folly upon the bridal couch, and lived treating the worker's thought as contemptible—and had triumphed. There was therefore nothing surprising in lovers of reform giving their own child Lenin's name! This revolutionary son, born to people of new ideas, too became known as Lenin.

One day Lenin went to the bank of the pond, stole the clothes of Brahmin women who were bathing, and climbed a tree; when they asked for their saris, he replied, “Krishna Leela.” It gave Mallika no small delight. Lenin's cheeks swelled with kisses! For Vipulanandar and Mallika, the love-talk of that day was all about his thought-provoking prank! Every act dripping with relish was Lenin's property! He would chase a pig running along the street into the house of a Vaishnavite devotee. As the devotee and his wife began to scream, he would start with, “O Dweller of Vaikuntha!” and expound the greatness of the Varaha avatar; and when Garuda was seen—**[the Tamil sentence continues onto scan 11 / Batch 2]**
