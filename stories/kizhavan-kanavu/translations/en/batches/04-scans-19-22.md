**[Continuation of the sentence begun on scan 18:]** —if the legs gave out—Mallika had come to such a state! Her suffering-filled life had been fashioned with turmoil and confusion as its mound, disappointment and yearning as its boundary line! His daughter's welfare was his own welfare—the desire that arose in her should be fulfilled through his own effort. Her life free from torment alone would be joy reaching the sky for him. Her tear-traces, and the turning pages of a sweet life—this was the joy old age would behold! This was the dream the old man had been dreaming. But his thought mingled with the earth. Markandeyar did not venture outside for many days. Mallika, however, had gone out into the world, and six months passed.

“With the gentle breeze beating the drum, the music of the sweet wind melting and pouring delight, the moon granting repose......” As words like these were being piled one upon another—

“Mallika! In those days, if we had sat upon the marble platform in the garden, would the world have failed to roar at the sight of you?”

As he asked this, a man touched Mallika. Who was that astonishing man who had recognized her? The sight of Mallika's face—once offering a smile joined with new thought—now so changed, was unbearable. And that was not all. How he recognized that worn figure, reclining on the raised platform of a choultry, as Mallika, no one knows.

<!-- source scan 20; printed page 16 -->

“Vipula! Is that you? You've gone and become a holy man!”

“No, Mallika. The marks of prison life.”

Yes. Vipulanandar's young beard had drawn that question from Mallika. She had sacrificed her father to Markandeyar's lust, begged from house to house to fill her stomach, waited for her lover's return, and lain in a choultry treating moonlight as poison; for her, the unexpected meeting with Vipulan bestowed a life of love. Was theirs merely a sham love? Had it not dawned at the summit of steadfastness? Vipulan, after completing his prison term, and Mallika had Lenin as the sign of the book of life. Lenin had crossed twelve years and was approaching his thirteenth. On the fortieth rung of life's ladder, Vipulanandar, a fire of revolution, sat with Mallika on the bank of the river of love, speaking words of reason and tasting the red sugarcane of reform that spurted delight. It was then that Lenin's youthful pranks were occurring with enough force to strike the heart. It was then, too, that one of his rationalist plays—the Vastrabharana drama at the pond—took place.

Before Mallika could say that Lenin had done this prank at the pond, her lips parted and her pearl-like teeth blossomed. Copper plates lay scattered—

**[Continuation from scan 21:]** —the uprising carried out by men! Sir Selvam, hidden by the Oman Sea! Thyagar! Nair! Panagal! Histories such as these became illuminating lights. “We lived—to be kissed by the sword. We bowed to the Aryans—we did not fall utterly—there! Our flag of liberation!” a lover told his beloved, and received a thousand kisses as the reward for those words. “Today is the day Dravidam was recovered! The Dravidian land is a day for Dravidians! Dravidian Festival Day!”—young men go along the street sounding their trumpets!

Vipulanandar and Mallika saw this dream in their minds! In the Dravidian realm, Lenin is a valiant citizen! This is the summit of their dream! As a memorial to the dream, more young comrades like Lenin are still needed!

Periyar EV Ramasamy too sees this very dream. The imperishable image of the Dravidam that is to come; that is the old man's dream.
