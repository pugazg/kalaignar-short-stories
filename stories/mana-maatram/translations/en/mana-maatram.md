# Change of Mind

> **English translation of the verified Tamil archival reading.** Controlling source: 1987 collection `கலைஞர் சொன்ன குட்டிக் கதைகள்`, scan **29 / printed page 28 → scan 30 / printed page 29**.

<!-- anthology scan: 29; printed page: 28 -->

A philanthropist and his wife are walking along a narrow single-file path. Their intention is to give a donation to someone.

A man is walking rapidly toward them along that path.

The philanthropist's wife says to him, “There he comes. Why not give this donation to him?” and hands the bundle of money in her hand to her husband.

The husband looks closely at the man and says, “Oh! Him? He is a man who often changes his mind! If we give it intending that he should receive it, he may fail to take it!” “Let us see even that,” says the wife.

“Very well, watch,” he says. He places the bundle of money on the path and says, “Let the two of us hide behind this tree and see whether the man coming this way picks up the money and takes it! If he gets this wealth, we shall regard it as good fortune that came to him and rejoice!” The two hide themselves.

<!-- anthology scan: 30; printed page: 29 -->

The bundle of money lies on the narrow path.

He comes quickly. As he comes, he has a change of mind.

Do you know what change of mind? “We are going so fast along this narrow path with our eyes open. Can we go a little distance with our eyes closed? Let us see,” he thinks, and walks with his eyes closed.

The very place through which he walks with his eyes closed is where the money bag lies. He passes right by it.

After he has gone, the philanthropist says to his wife, “Did you see? They say that what reaches the hand may not reach the mouth. See how exactly that applies to him. Because he changed his mind, what should have come to him failed to reach him.”

It is just like this with the tendency to change one's mind.

The people of Tamil Nadu are alert at other times. When an election comes, they change their minds and become weary.

## Translation note

The idiom `கைக்கு எட்டியது வாய்க்கு எட்டாது` is translated closely as “what reaches the hand may not reach the mouth,” preserving the source's rhetorical image.
