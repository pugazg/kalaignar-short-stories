The Congress view is that the states should not have greater powers. Our principle is that only when the states have greater authority can benefits be provided to the people of the states

<!-- source scan 28; printed page 26 -->

.

To tell a story here: at the entrance of a house, the daughter-in-law of that household is sitting with her knees drawn up. Seeing that she has not cooked even by one in the afternoon, a woman from the neighbouring house comes and asks, ‘Haven’t you cooked?’ She says, ‘No.’ ‘Why, is there no firewood?’ she asks. ‘There is firewood,’ she says. Then, ‘Is there no matchbox?’ she asks. ‘That too is there,’ the daughter-in-law says. ‘Is there no rice?’ the neighbour asks. ‘Do you need vegetables?’ she asks as well. ‘Everything is there,’ she says. When the neighbour asks, ‘Then why haven’t you cooked?’ the daughter-in-law says, “Everything is there. My mother-in-law put everything inside the room, locked it, took the key, tucked it at her waist, and went out!”

It is just like that in the country: everything is there. Tamil Nadu has all the facilities needed to build dams, provide irrigation facilities, and establish factories. But we alone do not have the authority to do those things. It is in the hands of the Central Government. The Dravidian movement is the movement striving to wrest that authority and use it to do good for the people of the states.
