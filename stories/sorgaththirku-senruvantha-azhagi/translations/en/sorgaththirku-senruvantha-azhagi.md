# The Beauty Who Went to Heaven and Returned!

> **English translation of the verified Tamil archival reading.** Controlling source: 1987 collection `கலைஞர் சொன்ன குட்டிக் கதைகள்`, lower scan **13 → 14 → upper 15**. Physical source-page boundaries are retained.

<!-- anthology scan: 13; printed page: 12; lower shared span -->

In a well-to-do family there lived a beautiful woman, admired by everyone and devoted to the path of chastity. She was her parents' only daughter. Suddenly she died. After her death, her mother and father lived in great sorrow, thinking only of her. They took out the ornaments they had happily adorned her with and, looking at them again and again, shed tears.

<!-- anthology scan: 14; printed page: 13 -->

One day the father had gone out and only the mother was at home. At that time an ascetic came to the house. The ascetic was a trickster—a cheat. The mother welcomed the false ascetic, attended to him, gave him food, and asked where he had come from. The false ascetic, who had come knowing that there were many jewels in the house, said, “I have come from heaven.”

At once the mother asked, “It has been three years since my daughter died. She too is in heaven, isn't she? Did you see her?”

The ascetic replied, “I saw her! I saw her! Your daughter is well. But she has only one grievance! When she went to heaven, she left all her jewels here. She told me, ‘Ascetic, if you go near my house, collect all of them and bring them to me!’”

When the ascetic said this, the mother exclaimed, “Did my daughter say that? Could my daughter be left without her jewels! Please give all these jewels to her.” She bundled up every jewel in the house and handed them to the ascetic. He took them and went away.

A little later the father returned from outside. The mother hurriedly and eagerly told her husband, “An ascetic came after seeing our daughter in heaven. He said our daughter was distressed because she had no jewels. I have sent all the jewels with him.” The father cried, “You wretch! Madwoman! You have been—”

<!-- anthology scan: 15; printed page: 14; upper shared span -->

“—deceived! Someone has thoroughly cheated you. So much for the ‘mothers' clan,’ see! You have been cheated like this!” Saying this, he immediately mounted his horse and chased the ascetic. The ascetic ran. The father, who had lost the jewels, pursued him relentlessly on horseback.

The ascetic kept running and suddenly climbed onto a tree branch and sat there. The man who had pursued him on horseback stopped the horse below the branch and climbed the tree to catch the ascetic. Seizing the moment, the ascetic on the branch jumped onto the horse and rode away.

Unable to catch him, the father said, “Ascetic! Give my daughter the jewels her mother gave you, and give her the horse I gave you as well,” and returned home in anguish.

“I ask you: should we, like the man in the story, return after giving away the horse too? Or should we recover the lost jewels—the lost rights—the lost feeling, and save the Tamil community that has been deceived by someone? Should we say, ‘Take the horse too’? That is the question I place before you.”

You are being cunningly deceived by someone! Those who continue to be engaged in the work of pointing this out. Even so, I myself have a doubt! Will even we one day become weary? That is my doubt. Will even we ourselves come to feel a mental distress? That is an uncertainty I have!

## Translation note

- The final rhetorical fragment is preserved as a fragment because the verified Tamil itself is structurally irregular.
- `தாய்க்குலம்` is rendered conservatively as “mothers' clan” rather than replaced by a smoother outside interpretation.
- The scan-14/scan-15 split occurs inside the father's exclamation and is retained at the corresponding English transition.
