A vulture, it is said, chased a dove. Seeking refuge, the dove flew here and there and finally fell into the hands of Emperor Sibi. He looked up and saw the vulture coming.

Sibi looked at the vulture and asked, “What do you want?” It said that it wanted the dove. “The dove has come to me seeking refuge. Therefore I cannot give it to you. If you wish, I will give you my own flesh in its place,” said Emperor Sibi. “All right, give it,” the vulture is said to have replied. Sibi placed the dove on one pan of a balance and cut flesh from his own body and placed it on the other. But the pan holding the dove kept sinking. Then, even after cutting flesh from his whole body and placing it on the scale, he could not balance it. So he himself climbed onto the balance and told the vulture, “Take me instead.” That is the story of Emperor Sibi. Abdul Rahman created a poem from it.

“O Goddess of Justice,
open your eyes a little
and look at the pans of your balance.
There—
the flesh of the dove
is now being eaten
by the Sibis.”

Through this award that we have presented to ‘Kavikko. Abdul Rahman, who fashioned such philosophies, beautiful imaginings, the condition of the country and the circumstances of human society in his natural Tamil, we take pride.
