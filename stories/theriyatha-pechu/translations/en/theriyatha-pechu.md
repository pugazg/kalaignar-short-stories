# Speaking Without Knowing

In the Soviet country, a young man was walking toward a mountain peak. As he walked, an elderly man of short stature came toward him.

<!-- source scan 46; printed page 44 -->

The elder looked at the boy and asked, “Why are you going to this mountain peak?” The boy replied:

“Lenin's house is on that mountain peak. I'm going to his house.” “Haven't you seen him?” the elder asked. “No; that's why I'm going to see him,” said the boy. “What is so special about him?” asked the elder.

“Everyone among the people loves him. That's why I'm going to see him,” said the very young boy.

“All right, go,” said the elder, and went away.

A short while after he had gone, a little girl by the roadside looked at the boy and asked, “What were you talking to Lenin about for so long?” The boy was astonished! Until then he had not known that the person he had been speaking with was Lenin himself. Only after the little girl said it did he realize: he had been talking with Lenin! He ran to look for him. By then the elder had hurried somewhere and disappeared.

‘Thus, without even knowing it was Lenin, he had been talking about Lenin. Likewise, today in India some people are talking about India without even knowing what India is. When it comes to Lenin,
