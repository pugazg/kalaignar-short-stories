Kannappa Nayanar’s birth name was Thinnan. The hunter called Thinnan was the one who became Kannappar. He regularly worshipped a Sivalingam in the forest. Even that worship was not conducted by offering vegetables. The very non-vegetarian food that our Madurai Adigalar would now refuse even to touch was what Kannappa Nayanar offered and used in his daily worship.

One day, blood flowed from the eye of that Sivalingam. He tried all kinds of herbs to treat it, but could not stop it. Then, using an arrow, he removed his own eye and placed it on the eye of the Sivalingam. Because he had thus placed an eye there, the one who had “applied an eye”—Kannappan—came to be so called.

All this ought to have been told by our Adigalar himself. I am turning into Adigalar and telling it to you. But that Sivalingam, instead of thinking, “He has given one eye; let each of us remain with one eye,” wished to test him and caused blood to flow from the other eye too. With no other way left, Kannappan prepared to pluck out his second eye and place it on the Sivalingam. But if he plucked out that eye too, he would not know where
