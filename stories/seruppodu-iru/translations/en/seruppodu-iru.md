# Stick to the Shoe

There was a painter in Greece. He could make very beautiful pictures. His practice was to paint pictures every day and then bring them out and place them at his doorway. One day he painted a majestic picture of a warrior and set it at the entrance to his house. He kept watching to see who looked at it and how they appreciated it. Many people passing that way looked at it and went on saying that the picture of the warrior was splendid.

Then a shoemaker came that way. He too looked at the picture. He looked at the warrior, looked at the sword, and looked at the shoe on the warrior's foot. Then he asked, “Where is the painter who painted this picture?” The painter immediately came over. “You haven't drawn the shoe correctly. There is a difference between this shoe and that shoe, isn't there?” the shoemaker asked. The painter too looked at it closely.

“Yes, what you say is right. I'll correct my mistake,” he said, and right there in front of him drew the shoe properly. The shoemaker said it was all right and went away.

The next day the painter painted a beautiful woman and, in the same way, put the picture on display in the street for others to see. The shoemaker came there again. After looking at it, could he simply keep quiet? At once he asked, “Where is the painter?” The painter came. Looking at him, the shoemaker said, “You've drawn this picture, but the pottu on the woman's forehead hasn't been placed correctly.” But the painter had in fact placed it correctly. So the painter became angry. Looking at the shoemaker, he said, “You stick to the shoe. Don't go beyond that.”
