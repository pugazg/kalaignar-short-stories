Udanpirappe....

I will first tell an imaginary story and then write this letter to you. You are eager to know what story it is, aren't you? A medley of a tale mixing puranam and thanam! In Yamaloka, Dharmarajan is seated in court. Why did Yama come to be called Dharmarajan? Is the one who kills human beings and carries away their lives to be called Dharmarajan? Such questions naturally arise! But the tellers of the puranas say that Yama is called Dharmarajan because he acts only out of the divine duty of taking away people's lives when their allotted lifespan ends, and does not deviate from dharma even by the slightest degree.

<!-- source scan 55; printed page 53 -->

Markandeyan was destined to die at the age of sixteen. So Yama came riding a buffalo, with his noose, to take his life. Frightened of death, Markandeyan ran into the temple at Thirukkadaiyur and embraced the Sivalingam. The Sivalingam burst open and Siva emerged from it and confronted Yama. Yama said that he had come to perform his duty. He argued that taking Markandeyan's life was his task under Lord Siva's own command. But Lord Siva granted an exception to his own order—brought about a ‘renaissance’ in Markandeyan's life—and made him remain sixteen forever. This too is a puranic story! Even according to this purana, Yama presents himself as Dharmarajan, bows to Siva, and returns.

In the court of that Yama called Dharmarajan, Chitrabuthiran presents two cases. See how Chitrabuthiran explains the first case to Yamadharman:

“Mahaprabhu! Here is this man, who lived for more than ninety years in the earthly world. With the support of the people he held high offices, sat as Chief Minister, and ruled. In the earthly world he also held an important responsibility in a political party. But because on two or three occasions he violated that party's discipline through his speech and actions, the party leadership had warned him. For that, he had also

expressed regret. But even after expressing such regret, he again and again began making needless complaints against the party leadership. When he was questioned about all that, he issued statements openly challenging the leadership itself. Now he says that he must be sent only to heaven. This entire court awaits Mahaprabhu's judgment.”

After hearing Chitrabuthiran's statement, Yamadharma Rajan says, “Oh! He may have held great offices. Nevertheless... nevertheless... he has violated party discipline. He has criticized the party leadership itself. Not only that, after first expressing regret for certain mistakes, he repeated the same mistake. If a man who violates discipline in this way is allowed to enter heaven, heaven too will violate discipline and become completely corrupted. Therefore I order that he be sent to hell. The fact that he held a high office and worked for the party cannot make his violation of party discipline acceptable. Therefore I issue a strict command that he be cast into hell so that he cannot see heaven even in his dreams.”

Following Yamadharmarajan's judgment, Chitrabuthiran sends the man to Narakaloka with a few kinkarars.

The next case is taken up. Once again Chitrabuthiran speaks:

“My lord! Mahaprabhu, this next case too is almost like the first one. Indeed, this man violated the discipline of the party to which he belonged, in speech and action, to an even greater degree than the man in the first case who has just received your judgment. The man in the first case at least merely found fault with the party leadership and criticized the leader. But this man declared that the party itself was his, that the flag belonged to him, that he had removed the party leadership; he passed resolutions to that effect, filed a case seeking to freeze the party's very symbol, lost that case, and then started a new party. Therefore I expect that you, Mahaprabhu, will give him the same judgment you gave in the first case.”

When Chitrabuthiran briefly set out the details of the case, Yamadharmarajan said, “Chitrabuthirar! Though the two cases you have explained are almost alike, my judgment is not going to be the same. I permit this man to remain in heaven.” Saying this, he rapidly descended the steps of his throne and embraced the man standing in the second case.

Astonished, Chitrabuthiran asked Yamadharmarajan with great emotion, “Mahaprabhu! What strange thing is this! People who committed the same kind of offence—

<!-- source scan 58; printed page 56 -->

and among them the second man behaved even more intensely in refusing to submit to the party leadership than the person judged first. Yet you have ordered hell for the first and heaven for the second. Can even you, Dharmarajan, go off the path?”

“Hey! Chitrabuthira! Are you casting a stain even on my judgment? I cannot tolerate this. Therefore the two of us can no longer continue acting together as we have until now.”

Thus Yamadharman flared up!

“Mahaprabhu, think a little! Two different judgments for the same kind of offence?” Chitrabuthiran again fired his arrow of a question!

“There is no bond or relationship between you and me anymore—go!” said Dharmarajan, and with his arm around the shoulder of the man bound for heaven, he left the royal hall.

Udanpirappe, I have finished my modern puranic story. Now read once the Politburo statement of the Marxist Communist Party!

“Last year, when Nirupan Chakraborty was receiving treatment in hospital, he gave an interview to a Bengali-language newspaper in Calcutta

Regarding this, the party's Central Committee asked the party's General Secretary to take stern action. When an explanation was sought on that basis, he gave an assurance that such a thing would not happen again in the future.

Again, while the party's 15th national conference was being held, he made slanderous statements about the party.

Because of this, the party's Central Committee asked the Politburo to take action. Taking into account Nirupan Chakraborty's service and sacrifice to the Communist movement, the party's Central Committee decided to remove him from elected positions and give him an opportunity to correct himself.

But instead of correcting his mistakes, Nirupan Chakraborty's making malicious and baseless accusations against the party and against party leaders—accusations that even enemies could not make—is regrettable.

“Nirupan Chakraborty has made accusations against the party that even enemies have not made. This is contrary to the party's rules of discipline. Therefore he is expelled from the party. No member is more important than the party. However great he may be, action will be taken against him.”
