That day’s ‘exhibition’ held no flavour for Annumalai, because no beauties had come. Annumalai was one who found fulfilment in the smiles that played upon the lips of queens of beauty. His doctrine was that unattractive women were unfit to live in this world. He often said that while Brahma was enjoying his time with Sarasaa, he had, in irritation at having to attend to the work of creation, fashioned a few lumps of flesh—and that those had become the unattractive women who troubled the world. To him life was a flower garden, and women were the parrots flying through it!

“Che! What kind of exhibition is this? An exhibition without beauties is fit only for a leprosy-stricken ‘Juliet’. Even Romeo wouldn’t touch her. They say the Tamil is the most handsome man in the world! Yet I cannot find one beautiful Tamil woman here......” Annumalai went on muttering like this. He cast his net through the bangle stalls and ribbon stalls. He cast his net for sel-kendai; only young varaal fish were caught. The women seen there did not seem concerned about how grotesque they looked—

—but somehow they had no hesitation in picking out beautiful bangles! Isn’t the world like that? If the cuckoo refused to sing because it was not beautiful...and if the peacock began to sing because it was beautiful...would not nature itself have changed?

Annumalai wandered about with a drooping heart. Hearing a ‘kaluk’ sound, he turned. Yes...the sel-kendai itself had been caught. In her wandering eyes he saw a wonder. With the bow of her brows she pierced him—akkathaaga—and killed him. Those lips like pomegranate blossoms stirred a storm of feeling in Annumalai’s heart. Would a woman who captivated all who saw her spare Annumalai?

Not only Annumalai; she captivated other men like him too with her smile. Every Monday Annumalai came and admired her from every angle. But that “poongothai” never moved away from the bangle stall! Whether beautiful woman, college student or Kamban’s Surpanakha, all of them are captivated by cloth merchants and sellers of bangles and ribbons. The difference is only in the “design” they choose; what they buy is still glass bangles and ‘imitation’ jewellery!

Yes! Women surrender what is genuine for imitation. So it was with Vimala: she surrendered her heart to Annumalai without realizing that he was an ‘imitation’, a polished piece of glass that merely gave a ‘dazzle’. Some men too are like women. They measure her beauty and her “make-up” bag, not her heart or her character. Some learn to make love; some train others in it. But do you know how many become victims of this?

Poor Vimala! She had fallen into Annumalai’s deep pit thinking it a cool stream. Nor was all the fault hers; what could she do? She was a plucked cluster of jasmine. The flower had been plucked from the plant, but not from its fragrance. It still gave off scent. Yes......she was a young widow. But her parents were intelligent, fearless people. That was why they had enrolled Vimala in college!

It was in that maiden’s college that she became caught by Annumalai. Vimala became prey to his habit of admiring beauty. Without knowing Vimala’s foolish condition, Annumalai went hunting beauties; while he was still smelling one cluster of jasmine, he desired another rose. There Annumalai committed a great killing. Yes! He crushed that flower.

Poor Vimala was ruined. In another nine months she would become the mother of a child.

Now Annumalai no longer cared about Vimala. After all, how many Vimalas were available to him in the ‘market’!

Vimala cried; she rolled about in anguish! But the result...? Annumalai would not even turn back to look at her. When class ended he would rise and leave by another side without seeing her.

Does not a parrot leap from tree to tree? Whichever tree is green, whichever tree has more fruit—to that tree the green parrot jumps. Annumalai was the same! He leapt wherever he saw a beauty. He was a thothu-kili. Should not the tree have examined beforehand whether this was a parrot that would stay there permanently? Vimala failed to think; alas, she suffered.

Slowly Vimala understood Annumalai’s secret. She became a tigress! Before her eyes Kannagi—

—and Madhavi came and went again and again. Manimekalai appeared with the Amudha Surabhi. She saw Nallathangal attempting suicide. Her body broke into sweat once, then turned cold. Blood rushed from her heart with new force through her whole body.

“Isn’t it because he is handsome that I fell into his net and now struggle? Isn’t it because he is handsome that he ruins so many women? If his beauty were destroyed....?” Thinking many things, Vimala went to college.

‘Chemistry practical’ was in progress. The professor was questioning a boy about a glass tube he had broken. Vimala worked at her experiment, straining and groaning. When people asked what was wrong, she said she had stomach pain. Only she knew what had happened and what was about to happen.

Annumalai was conducting an experiment in one corner. Unlike before, he paid no attention to Vimala now. Vimala slowly moved from her place. Does not a lamp about to go out burn with sudden brightness? In the same way there was a light, a small joy, on her face. She held a ‘bottle’ in her hand. None of the students noticed Vimala.

Vimala slowly opened the bottle. She hesitated a little. Once one is about to fall into a pit of fire, should one fear the heat? Once one has decided to leap into the sea, should the waves shake the mind? She poured what was in the bottle onto Annumalai’s face. With an “Ah!” Annumalai screamed and fell. His face was seared and scorched. At the same moment Vimala too collapsed to the floor. Yes! Vimala poured “nitric acid” onto Annumalai’s face so that his beauty would die. She too drank the fiery liquid—enough to tear through the gut!

In the chemistry laboratory one woman was gaining release from this world. One man—the lotus-faced Annumalai, who with his beauty had ruined beauties—lay below, disfigured. From now on womankind would not even turn to look at him; it would kick him aside.

None of the students understood anything; they stood stunned. Only that thothukkili Annumalai knew the reason. Before him stood the revolutionary poet of Puduvai, smiling. His lips moved. “Dear child! Today women on earth are no longer in a lowly state; they have awakened! You thought you could play games with the women of the town! Wicked man, enter into your conscience! Do not stand there!” The poet disappeared. Annumalai squeezed his eyes tightly shut, unable to bear the burning.
