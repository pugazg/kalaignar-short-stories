# Narayana! Narayana!

> **English translation of the verified Tamil archival reading.** Controlling source: 1987 collection `கலைஞர் சொன்ன குட்டிக் கதைகள்`, scan **8 → 9 → upper 10**. Physical source-page boundaries are retained.

<!-- anthology scan: 8; printed page: 7 -->

Narada travels through many parts of the earthly world and comes to a tiny village. There he sees a poor farmer working in a field. As Narada approaches, he keeps saying, “Narayana, Narayana.” Seeing the farmer, Narada says, “I travel all over the world. Without rest my lips keep chanting ‘Narayana, Narayana.’ But you are merely working here in this field. Should you not say ‘Narayana, Narayana’? Will your sins not disappear? Will merit not come to you?”

<!-- anthology scan: 9; printed page: 8 -->

The farmer immediately took Narada to his house and said, “I will give you a task. First do that. Then I will clear the doubt you have raised.” Narada asked what the task was. The farmer poured oil into a small bowl, placed the bowl in Narada's hand, and said, “Sage Narada! Hold this bowl of oil in your hand and go once around this village.” Narada held the bowl in his hand and went around the village.

When he had completed the circuit, the farmer asked, “You went around the village. While going around with this bowl of oil in your hand, how many times did you say ‘Narayana, Narayana’?” Narada replied, “I did not say it at all.” When the farmer asked why, Narada said, “All my attention was on the bowl of oil. I was anxious that I should bring the bowl back without spilling or scattering the oil. That is why I did not say Narayana! Narayana!”

It is the same for us. Like the bowl of oil placed in Narada's hand, we have the rights of the state and the problems of the state; the thought that the Tamil language in this state must be kept alive; the thought that the rights of the Tamil must be protected; the thought that the dignity of our people must be protected; the thought that Tamil Nadu must be made a bastion of rationalism. Because all these are like the bowl of oil in our hands, in our concern to carry it without spilling or scattering it, we did not say “Nationalism!”—

<!-- anthology scan: 10; printed page: 9; upper shared span -->

—we did not say “Nationalism.” Just as Narada forgot Narayana! Narayana!, we too, without worrying about nationalism, have been worrying about ourselves, about our society, and about the language. Without understanding this, some nationalists speak as though nationalism were heaven and as though speaking about our state's relations would take us to utter hell. That is not proper!
