As we offer our wishes to the newlyweds, history gives us two examples of how a family—especially a family involved in public life—ought to be. As for the seed of communism in the world—

<!-- source scan 38; printed page 36 -->

the great scholar Karl Marx was the reason it was planted. His incomparable book is one that is read and followed today by the working class throughout the world. When we think of how many obstacles and hardships Karl Marx, who wrote that book and lived for that principle, had to endure, history tells us that Marx's wife lived with him to the very end, meeting all those hardships and bearing all the troubles that came to her husband.

Likewise, we read about Socrates, the philosopher of Greece. He devoted himself entirely to the people, and wherever he met people, that old man never failed to tell them his ideas. Standing at the entrance to his house, he would stop everyone passing along the street and keep pouring out his ideas and philosophy. But his wife was exactly the opposite. Her voice would be heard from inside: she would scold him and abuse him. “Don't you have any other work to do?” she would shout. Those who had come to hear Socrates speak would apparently ask, “What's that noise inside?” Socrates would reply, “Nothing. It's thunder.” His wife, after putting up with it for some time, would come outside with a bucket of water and pour it over Socrates’

head. Then the people would ask, “What's this?” Socrates would say, “Didn't it thunder? The rain is following it.”

I have told you both the history of a great man who remained engaged in public life while accommodating his wife in this way, and the history of Karl Marx, whose wife and husband together bore every kind of obstacle in public life while running their family, so that both examples may be understood. I ask the newlyweds, Madhi Oli and Prince, to understand which kind of life they should follow and conduct their own life accordingly, and once again I convey my wishes to the newlyweds.
